What is Testosterone Cypionate?

Testosterone cypionate is a synthetic form of testosterone, the primary male sex hormone. It's given as an intramuscular injection and is one of the longest-acting testosterone formulations available.

Common Uses

Doctors prescribe testosterone cypionate for:

  • Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) for transgender men and non-binary individuals
  • Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) for cisgender men with low testosterone
  • Delayed puberty in adolescents
  • Muscle wasting from certain medical conditions
  • Hypogonadism (abnormally low testosterone levels)

How It Works

Testosterone cypionate dissolves slowly in body fat after injection, releasing testosterone gradually over 1 to 2 weeks. This steady release means fewer injections compared to shorter-acting forms.

Dosage

Dosages vary based on individual needs and response. Typical ranges are 50 to 400 mg per injection, given every 1 to 4 weeks. Your provider will adjust dosage based on blood tests and your symptoms.

Potential Side Effects

Common side effects include acne, mood changes, and fluid retention. Less common but serious effects can include blood clots, liver problems, and heart issues. Regular monitoring with blood work is essential.

Accessing Testosterone Cypionate via Telehealth

Many telehealth providers now offer testosterone replacement therapy consultations and prescriptions. You'll typically need:

  • An initial video consultation with a licensed provider
  • Blood work to check baseline hormone levels
  • Follow-up appointments to monitor your progress

Some states have specific regulations around HRT prescribing, so availability varies by location.
