Online pharmacies range from legitimate operations that fill prescriptions safely to unlicensed vendors selling counterfeit or substandard drugs. The difference matters to your wallet and your health.

What makes an online pharmacy legitimate

Licensed online pharmacies require a valid prescription, have a pharmacist available to answer questions, and are licensed in the state where they operate. Check for a NABP Verified Internet Pharmacy Practice Sites seal, which signals they've met state pharmacy board standards.

Price varies, sometimes sharply

Online pharmacies often undercut brick-and-mortar locations because they have lower overhead. Prices for the same medication can differ by 50% or more between providers. It's worth comparing three or four sites if you're paying out-of-pocket, but make sure you're comparing the same drug, dose, and quantity.

Watch for red flags

Avoid any online pharmacy that sells prescription drugs without requiring a prescription, offers medications at prices that seem unrealistic, or has no licensed pharmacist listed. Never buy from sites that spam your email or pop up as ads promising miracle cures.

Shipping takes time

Most legitimate online pharmacies take 5 to 10 business days to process and ship orders. If someone promises next-day delivery on a prescription, that's a warning sign.

Your insurance might not cover it

Check whether your pharmacy plan covers mail-order or online prescriptions before you order. Some plans require you to use their preferred mail-order service or face higher out-of-pocket costs.
