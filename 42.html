What Disulfiram Does

Disulfiram is a medication that treats alcohol use disorder by making you physically ill if you drink alcohol. It doesn't reduce cravings or treat withdrawal. Instead, it creates a strong disincentive to drink by blocking the breakdown of alcohol in your body, causing acetaldehyde to build up and trigger an unpleasant reaction.

How It Works

When you take disulfiram daily, it inhibits the enzyme aldehyde dehydrogenase. If you then consume alcohol, acetaldehyde accumulates and produces:

  • Facial flushing and chest pain
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Headache and dizziness
  • Hypotension and tachycardia
  • In severe cases, cardiovascular collapse

This reaction typically starts within 5 to 10 minutes of drinking and lasts several hours.

Who It's For

Disulfiram works best for people who:

  • Have completed detoxification
  • Want a behavioural deterrent while building recovery skills
  • Can commit to daily dosing
  • Understand they must avoid all alcohol, including in food, medicine, and mouthwash

Important Considerations

Disulfiram is not a cure. It requires motivation and commitment. You must avoid any source of alcohol, and you should not start it until you've been alcohol-free for at least 12 to 24 hours. Liver problems can affect how safely you take it, so your doctor will likely run blood tests first.

It works alongside counselling and other support, not instead of them.
