Weight loss medications have shifted from niche diabetes drugs to mainstream treatments. Semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) and tirzepatide (Zepbound, Mounjaro) are now prescribed specifically for weight loss, not just blood sugar control.

What's Different Now

These GLP-1 receptor agonists work by slowing stomach emptying and signalling fullness to your brain. The difference: they're being dosed and marketed differently for weight loss than they were for diabetes. The doses are higher. The indication is weight loss itself, not a secondary benefit.

What They Cost

Out of pocket without insurance: $900 to $1,500 monthly for semaglutide or tirzepatide. With insurance, copays typically range from $50 to $200 per injection, depending on your plan. Many insurers now cover these drugs for weight loss if you meet criteria: usually a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 with weight-related conditions like high blood pressure or sleep apnea.

How to Get One

You need a prescription. That means seeing a doctor: your primary care doctor, a weight loss specialist, or through a telehealth service that prescribes weight loss medications. Telehealth options have expanded availability and often shorter wait times, though not all plans accept insurance.

Check your insurance first. If it covers the medication, your doctor can verify coverage before prescribing. If you're uninsured or your plan doesn't cover it, direct-pay telehealth services cost $100 to $300 for the initial visit plus medication cost.

Wait times matter. Some practices have months-long queues. Telehealth services typically get you in within days.
