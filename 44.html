Duloxetine is a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, or SNRI. It's used to treat depression, anxiety, nerve pain and fibromyalgia.

How it works

Duloxetine increases levels of serotonin and norepinephrine in your brain. These neurotransmitters regulate mood, pain perception and stress response.

What it treats

Duloxetine is approved by the FDA for:

  • Major depressive disorder
  • Generalised anxiety disorder
  • Diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain
  • Fibromyalgia
  • Chronic musculoskeletal pain
  • Chronic pain from osteoarthritis

Doctors also prescribe it off-label for stress urinary incontinence and other conditions.

Typical dosing

Duloxetine comes as 20 mg, 30 mg and 60 mg capsules. Doses range from 30 mg to 120 mg daily, depending on the condition being treated and how you respond.

Most people start at a lower dose and increase gradually. It typically takes 2 to 4 weeks to notice improvement.

Common side effects

  • Nausea
  • Dry mouth
  • Dizziness
  • Insomnia
  • Fatigue
  • Constipation

Side effects often improve after the first few weeks of treatment.

Important interactions

Duloxetine can interact with:

  • Other antidepressants
  • MAO inhibitors
  • Certain pain medications
  • Blood thinners
  • NSAIDs

Always tell your doctor or pharmacist about other medications you're taking.

Withdrawal and stopping

Do not stop duloxetine suddenly. Your doctor will typically lower your dose gradually over at least one to two weeks to avoid withdrawal symptoms like dizziness, headache or mood changes.
