Buprenorphine-naloxone, sold as Suboxone, is a combination medication that treats opioid use disorder. It pairs buprenorphine, a partial opioid agonist, with naloxone, an opioid antagonist.

How it works

Buprenorphine reduces cravings and withdrawal symptoms by binding weakly to opioid receptors. Naloxone is included to discourage misuse; it only activates if someone tries to inject the medication.

Forms and dosing

You'll find buprenorphine-naloxone as sublingual films, tablets, and oral solutions. Doses range from 2/0.5 mg to 24/6 mg per day, adjusted based on your response to treatment.

What to expect

Most people start feeling effects within 30 to 60 minutes. Treatment typically lasts months to years. You'll need regular check-ins with your provider to monitor progress and adjust dosage.

Side effects

Common ones include headache, constipation, insomnia, and nausea. Most settle within the first few weeks.

Cost

Prices vary widely by form, dose, and insurance. Generic versions cost less than brand-name Suboxone. Many insurance plans cover it, and cash prices typically run $5 to $15 per dose.
