Finasteride: What It Does and How It Works for Hair Loss

Finasteride is an oral medication that treats male pattern baldness by blocking DHT, the hormone responsible for shrinking hair follicles. It's sold under brand names like Propecia (prescription strength) and Rogaine (over-the-counter lower dose).

How It Works

Male pattern baldness happens when hair follicles shrink in response to DHT. Finasteride stops this process by blocking the enzyme that converts testosterone into DHT. This slows hair loss and can regrow some hair in certain cases.

What to Expect

Results take time. Most people need 3 to 6 months of daily use before seeing any change. Hair regrowth, when it happens, is usually modest. You need to keep taking it to maintain results, or hair loss resumes.

Key Facts

  • Available by prescription (1mg tablets) or over-the-counter (lower doses)
  • Taken once daily by mouth
  • Works best for hair loss on the crown and mid-scalp, less effective at the hairline
  • Must be used continuously; benefits stop after you stop taking it
  • Generally well-tolerated, though some men report side effects affecting sexual function

Getting Finasteride

You can get finasteride through:

  • Your primary care doctor or a dermatologist
  • Telehealth providers who specialise in hair loss
  • Some online pharmacies with a prescription

Telehealth visits for finasteride are often faster and cheaper than in-person appointments, though you'll still need a valid prescription.
