NAD+ is a coenzyme present in every living cell. It plays a role in energy production and DNA repair, among other functions. Levels naturally decline with age.

What NAD+ does

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) shuttles electrons during cellular energy production. It also activates sirtuins, proteins involved in DNA repair and cell survival under stress.

Research in animals suggests NAD+ decline contributes to ageing and age-related disease. Human evidence remains limited.

Supplementation options

Common forms include nicotinamide riboside (NR), nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), niacin and tryptophan. Oral supplements are available over the counter. IV NAD+ infusions are offered by some clinics.

Oral forms are cheaper. IV forms claim faster absorption but lack strong human evidence for superiority.

What research shows

Animal studies show promise for age-related conditions and metabolic health. Human trials are small and mostly short-term. No supplement has FDA approval for any disease.

Common marketing claims (anti-ageing, cognitive enhancement, longevity) outpace the current evidence.

Cost and access

Oral supplements: typically 20 to 50 dollars per month.

IV infusions: typically 200 to 500 dollars per session.

Testing NAD+ levels is available through some private labs and functional medicine clinics, though clinical utility remains unclear.

The bottom line

NAD+ supplementation is not a proven treatment for any condition. If considering it, discuss it with your doctor, especially if you take other medications or have underlying health conditions.
