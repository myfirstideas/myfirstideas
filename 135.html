Zepbound (tirzepatide) runs $1,350 per month at full retail price. No insurance means you pay that, unless you use a discount program or shop around.

Discount Programs Cut the Cost

Eli Lilly offers a coupon that caps your out-of-pocket cost at $550 per month for the full dose. You fill it at a regular pharmacy.

GoodRx and similar platforms let you compare prices across pharmacies. You might pay $900 to $1,200 depending on location and which pharmacy has stock.

Patient Assistance Programs

Eli Lilly's Lilly Cares program can provide Zepbound free or low-cost if you meet income requirements. Household income limits are roughly 300% of the federal poverty line.

Online Pharmacies and Telehealth

Some online telehealth services offer Zepbound at lower prices, sometimes $700 to $900 per month. These usually bundle a doctor visit with the prescription. Check that the platform is licensed and the doctor is real.

Wegovy as an Alternative

Wegovy (semaglutide) has more discount options and sometimes costs less out-of-pocket. Novo Nordisk's patient program caps costs at $250 per month for some people.

The Bottom Line

Start with Eli Lilly's $550 coupon. Check GoodRx for local pharmacy prices. Apply for patient assistance if your income qualifies. Going uninsured for Zepbound does not mean paying full price.
