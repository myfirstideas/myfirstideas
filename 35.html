BPC-157 is a synthetic peptide derived from a protective protein found in gastric juice. It's not approved by the FDA and is not available as a prescription medication in the United States.

What researchers are studying

Laboratory and animal studies suggest BPC-157 may play a role in:

  • Wound healing and tissue repair
  • Gut barrier function
  • Nerve growth and protection
  • Blood vessel formation

These early findings have generated interest in the research community, but human clinical trials remain limited.

Current availability

BPC-157 is sold as a research chemical or dietary supplement in some online marketplaces. It is not regulated as a medication by the FDA, which means:

  • Quality and purity are not guaranteed
  • Safety in humans is not established
  • Efficacy claims are not proven
  • There is no standardised dosing information

What you should know

If you are considering BPC-157, talk with your healthcare provider first. The gap between laboratory findings and proven human benefit is significant. Your doctor can discuss what the current evidence actually supports and what risks may apply to you personally.
