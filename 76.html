What is paroxetine?

Paroxetine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) antidepressant. It works by increasing serotonin levels in the brain to improve mood, reduce anxiety and help manage obsessive or intrusive thoughts.

What is it used for?

Paroxetine treats:

  • Major depressive disorder
  • Generalized anxiety disorder
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)
  • Panic disorder
  • Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
  • Social anxiety disorder

Doctors also prescribe it off-label for other conditions.

How does paroxetine work?

SSRIs like paroxetine block the reabsorption of serotonin in the brain. This keeps serotonin active longer, which can lift mood and reduce anxiety symptoms over time.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, headache, drowsiness, insomnia, sexual dysfunction and weight changes. Most side effects improve within the first few weeks as your body adjusts.

Getting paroxetine through telehealth

Many telehealth providers can diagnose depression, anxiety and other conditions paroxetine treats, then prescribe it if appropriate. Some telehealth services focus on mental health and have psychiatrists or psychiatric nurse practitioners available.

Cost and insurance

Paroxetine is generic and affordable, usually 10 to 30 dollars for a month's supply without insurance. Most insurance plans cover it.

For more details on paroxetine dosing, interactions and whether it's right for you, speak with your doctor or mental health provider.
