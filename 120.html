How to Find the Cheapest Cash Price for a Prescription

When you're paying out of pocket for medication, every dollar counts. Here's how to find the lowest price.

Check Multiple Pharmacies

Prices vary wildly between chains and independent pharmacies. Call or use their websites to compare costs for your specific medication. Don't assume the pharmacy you use for other things has the best price on everything.

Use Prescription Discount Programs

GoodRx, SingleCare and similar platforms let you compare prices and apply discounts without needing insurance. Many offer free coupons that work at most major pharmacies. The savings can be substantial: 30% to 70% off is common.

Ask About Generic Versions

Generic medications contain the same active ingredients as brand-name drugs but cost significantly less. If your prescription is for a brand name, ask your doctor or pharmacist whether a generic is available.

Request a Price Match

Some pharmacies will match a lower price you've found elsewhere. It's worth asking, especially at independent pharmacies that have more flexibility on pricing.

Check Manufacturer Programs

Pharmaceutical companies often offer patient assistance programs for their medications. Eligibility usually depends on income. Your doctor's office or the manufacturer's website can point you to these programs.

Compare Mail Order and Online Pharmacies

Online pharmacies sometimes undercut brick-and-mortar locations, particularly for medications you take regularly. Make sure any pharmacy you use is licensed and accredited.
