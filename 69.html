What Naltrexone Does

Naltrexone is an opioid antagonist, which means it blocks opioid and alcohol from activating receptors in your brain. It doesn't get you high and isn't addictive itself.

How It Works

The medication binds to opioid receptors without activating them. This blocks the rewarding effects of opioids and alcohol, reducing cravings and the reinforcement that drives continued use.

What It Treats

Naltrexone is prescribed for two main conditions:

  • Opioid use disorder
  • Alcohol use disorder

Forms and Dosing

You can get naltrexone as a daily pill or as a monthly injection (Vivitrol). The injection form may improve compliance since you don't have to remember a daily dose.

Who It's For

Naltrexone works best for people ready to stop using opioids or alcohol and looking for medication to reduce cravings during recovery. It's not a substitute for therapy or support programs.

Important Considerations

You must be fully detoxified from opioids before starting naltrexone, or you'll experience withdrawal. Tell your doctor about any pain medications you take, since naltrexone will block their effects.
