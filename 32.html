What is Atomoxetine?

Atomoxetine is a non-stimulant medication used to treat attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children, teenagers, and adults. Unlike stimulant medications, it works by increasing norepinephrine levels in the brain rather than dopamine and norepinephrine together.

How Atomoxetine Works

Atomoxetine is a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (NRI). It blocks the reabsorption of norepinephrine, a neurotransmitter that helps with focus, attention, and impulse control. This action allows norepinephrine to remain active in the brain longer, improving ADHD symptoms.

Who Takes Atomoxetine

Atomoxetine is prescribed for ADHD in people aged 6 and older. It's often chosen when:

  • Stimulant medications aren't effective or cause unwanted side effects
  • A patient has a history of substance abuse or addiction concerns
  • Heart problems or high blood pressure exist
  • Someone prefers a non-stimulant option

Common Side Effects

Common side effects include nausea, reduced appetite, dizziness, tiredness, and mood swings. Most side effects decrease over time as your body adjusts. Serious but rare side effects include liver damage and severe allergic reactions.

Atomoxetine and Telehealth

Many telehealth providers can prescribe and manage atomoxetine treatment through virtual visits. Follow-up appointments help monitor effectiveness and side effects without requiring in-person clinic visits.
