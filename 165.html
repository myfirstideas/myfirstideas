Peptides for Hair Growth: The Facts

Peptides have entered the hair loss conversation as an alternative to finasteride and minoxidil. The evidence is thinner than for those established treatments, but some peptides show promise in early research.

Which Peptides Have Evidence

The peptides with the most research backing are:

  • Copper peptides: shown to increase hair thickness in some studies, though results are modest
  • BPC-157: preliminary evidence suggests possible hair growth effects, but human trials are limited
  • Thymosin beta-4: animal studies show promise, but human data is sparse

None of these have the level of clinical evidence that supports minoxidil or finasteride for hair loss.

What You'll Pay

Peptide treatments typically run between 200 and 600 dollars per month, depending on the peptide, dosage, and provider. Most insurance does not cover peptide therapy for hair loss, as it remains off-label use.

Finding a Legitimate Provider

Look for providers who:

  • Require a consultation before prescribing
  • Disclose pricing upfront
  • Source peptides from licensed compounding pharmacies
  • Can explain the evidence (and limitations) clearly
  • Do not make promises about results

Telehealth platforms now offer peptide consultations, but verify that prescribers are licensed physicians in your state and that any compounded medications comply with local pharmacy regulations.

The Bottom Line

Peptides may help some people, but they're not proven at the scale finasteride and minoxidil are. If you're considering them, start with established treatments first and use peptides as an addition only after discussing options with a licensed provider.
