Sublingual immunotherapy, or SLIT, is an allergy treatment you take by mouth instead of by injection. You place a tablet under your tongue where it dissolves, introducing small amounts of allergen to help your immune system build tolerance over time.

How sublingual immunotherapy works

SLIT works through gradual exposure. The allergen tablet dissolves under your tongue, where specialized immune cells absorb it. Your immune system responds by building tolerance, reducing how strongly it reacts to that allergen in daily life. This process typically takes months to show results, but can last years after treatment stops.

SLIT vs immunotherapy shots

Sublingual tablets and allergy shots both use immunotherapy, but differ in delivery and convenience. Tablets go under your tongue at home; shots require office visits. Tablets have fewer serious side effects but work more slowly. Shots tend to produce stronger immune responses but carry higher risk of severe reactions.

Conditions SLIT treats

Sublingual immunotherapy is approved for:

  • Grass pollen allergy
  • Ragweed pollen allergy
  • Dust mite allergy
  • Tree pollen allergy

Your allergist can determine if SLIT suits your specific allergies.

What the evidence shows

Research shows SLIT reduces allergy symptoms by 20 to 40 percent in most people, with benefits emerging after 6 to 12 months of treatment. Long-term use appears safe for both adults and children aged 5 and up.
