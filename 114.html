Most major health insurers now cover telehealth visits, but coverage isn't uniform across all plans or states.

Coverage varies by insurer and plan type

Major insurers including UnitedHealthcare, Anthem, Aetna and Cigna all offer telehealth benefits to at least some members. Coverage terms differ: some plans cover telehealth at the same cost-share as in-person visits, others charge different copays or coinsurance.

What affects your coverage

Your telehealth coverage depends on several factors:

  • Your specific health plan (even within the same insurer, coverage can differ between plans)
  • Your state of residence and provider location
  • The type of provider delivering care (licensed physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants)
  • Whether the visit is for a new or established concern
  • The telehealth platform or provider used

Check your plan documents

The best way to confirm coverage is to contact your insurer directly or review your plan's summary of benefits and coverage (SBC). Ask specifically about:

  • Which telehealth services are covered
  • Any copay or coinsurance amounts
  • Which providers or platforms are in-network
  • Whether prior authorization is needed

Many insurers now offer telehealth at reduced cost compared to urgent care or emergency room visits, making it a financially practical option for many people.
