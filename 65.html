What is methylphenidate?

Methylphenidate is a prescription stimulant medication used to treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and narcolepsy. It works by increasing dopamine and norepinephrine activity in the brain, helping improve focus, attention, and impulse control.

How methylphenidate is taken

Methylphenidate comes in multiple forms:

  • Immediate-release tablets: Effects begin within 30 to 60 minutes and last 4 to 6 hours
  • Extended-release capsules and tablets: Effects last 8 to 12 hours
  • Liquid suspension: For patients who need dose flexibility
  • Chewable tablets: Easier administration for some users

Dosing depends on individual response and tolerability. Your clinician will start low and adjust based on effectiveness and side effects.

Common side effects

Most side effects are mild and often decrease over time:

  • Reduced appetite
  • Sleep problems
  • Headache
  • Increased heart rate or blood pressure
  • Anxiety or nervousness

Serious side effects are rare but can include chest pain, severe headache, or signs of allergic reaction.

Cost and coverage

Methylphenidate is a generic medication, making it significantly cheaper than brand-name alternatives like Ritalin or Concerta. Most insurance plans cover it. Typical costs range from $20 to $60 per month with insurance; uninsured prices vary by pharmacy and dose.

Who should avoid methylphenidate

Methylphenidate is not suitable for people with:

  • Heart conditions or high blood pressure
  • Glaucoma
  • Thyroid problems
  • Severe anxiety or agitation
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding

Always disclose your full medical history to your prescriber.

Getting started with methylphenidate

You can pursue treatment through traditional in-person appointments or telehealth visits, which offer flexibility for ongoing monitoring and dose adjustments. Many virtual clinics specialise in ADHD diagnosis and treatment.
