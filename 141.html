Sermorelin vs Ipamorelin: A Side-by-Side Look

Both sermorelin and ipamorelin trigger your body to release more growth hormone. Both come as injections. Both are peptides. But they're not identical, and choosing between them depends on what you're trying to achieve and how your body responds.

How They Work

Sermorelin mimics GHRH (growth hormone-releasing hormone). It signals your pituitary gland to produce and release growth hormone. Ipamorelin works differently: it mimics ghrelin, a hormone that also prompts growth hormone release, but through a separate pathway. This matters because your body may respond better to one signal than the other.

Key Differences

Sermorelin

  • Closer to your body's natural process
  • Requires multiple injections daily for most protocols
  • May take 3 to 6 months to see meaningful results
  • Typically costs $100 to $300 per month
  • Can trigger appetite as a side effect

Ipamorelin

  • More selective (targets only growth hormone release)
  • Often used in combination with sermorelin for better results
  • Similar timeline to benefits
  • Usually costs $150 to $400 per month
  • Less likely to affect appetite

Which One to Choose

Sermorelin makes sense if you want a more physiological approach and don't mind appetite changes. Ipamorelin suits you if you prefer a targeted action and want to avoid appetite stimulation. Many patients see the best results by combining both, though this increases cost and complexity.

Start with a telehealth consultation to discuss your goals, baseline hormone levels, and any health factors that might influence your choice. A provider can order blood work to guide the decision.

Cost and Access

Both peptides are available through telehealth providers, though they're not typically covered by insurance. Budget several months of consistent use before expecting noticeable changes in muscle tone, energy or recovery.
