How to Choose a Telehealth Provider: What Matters

Picking a telehealth provider feels like it should be simple. It's not, because the field is crowded and the trade-offs aren't obvious until you're inside them.

The Four Things That Actually Matter

Licensing and credentials. Your provider needs a valid license in your state. Full stop. Check the medical board website if you're not certain. This isn't a nice-to-have.

How long you wait. Some platforms get you to a clinician in hours. Others take days. Know what you need before you sign up. Acute problems need speed. Routine refills don't.

What it costs. Telehealth pricing runs from flat per-visit fees ($50 to $150) to subscription models to insurance billing. Your out-of-pocket number depends on what you have and what the provider accepts. Get a quote before you commit.

Insurance coverage. Not all telehealth platforms work with your plan. Ask directly. Don't assume. The platforms that take insurance usually have a checker on their site.

One More Layer: What You're Actually Using It For

A platform built for mental health might feel clunky for dermatology. A provider network strong in your region might have gaps elsewhere. Match the platform to what you need, not the other way around.

Start with your use case. Then run through the four questions above. You'll narrow the field fast.
