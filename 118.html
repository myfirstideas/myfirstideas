How to verify an online pharmacy is legitimate

Fake online pharmacies cost Americans over 100 million dollars a year. They sell counterfeit pills, expired stock and sometimes nothing at all.

Here's how to check an online pharmacy is real before you order.

1. Look for a valid pharmacy license

Legitimate online pharmacies display their state pharmacy license number on the website. Search your state pharmacy board's website to verify the license is active and the pharmacy address matches.

2. Check for NABP accreditation

The National Association of Boards of Pharmacy (NABP) vets online pharmacies. Look for the NABP Verified seal or check the NABP-verified pharmacy list. This is the gold standard.

3. Require a valid prescription

A pharmacy that sells prescription medications without asking for a prescription is breaking the law. If a site lets you buy without one, it's not legitimate.

4. Verify the pharmacist is licensed

Every prescription must be reviewed by a licensed pharmacist before it ships. The pharmacy should list the pharmacist's name and license number.

5. Check the physical address

Legitimate pharmacies have a real street address. Use Google Maps to verify it exists. Avoid pharmacies that only list a PO box or have no address at all.

Red flags to watch for

Don't buy from a pharmacy that:

  • Uses high-pressure sales tactics
  • Sells prescription drugs without a valid prescription
  • Offers prices that seem too low to be real
  • Has no licensed pharmacist on staff
  • Won't provide contact information
  • Refuses to answer questions about their credentials
