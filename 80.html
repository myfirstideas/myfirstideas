What rapamycin does

Rapamycin is an immunosuppressant medication that works by inhibiting mTOR, a protein that controls cell growth and division. It's used to prevent rejection of transplanted organs and to treat certain types of cancer and rare skin conditions.

Common uses

Rapamycin is prescribed for:

  • Prevention of organ rejection after kidney, heart, or liver transplant
  • Treatment of advanced renal cell carcinoma
  • Treatment of pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors
  • Lymphangioleiomyomatosis, a rare lung condition
  • Certain other rare conditions involving abnormal cell growth

How it works

Rapamycin suppresses the immune system by blocking mTOR signalling, which reduces the activity of T and B lymphocytes. This prevents the body from attacking transplanted organs. In cancer treatment, the same mechanism slows or stops the growth of cancer cells.

Side effects and considerations

Common side effects include increased infection risk, high cholesterol, mouth sores, and nausea. Because rapamycin affects immune function, patients need regular blood work and monitoring for signs of infection.

Rapamycin can interact with many medications, so inform your healthcare provider of all drugs and supplements you take.

Access and cost

Rapamycin is available by prescription only. It's typically covered by insurance for transplant rejection prevention, though coverage for other uses varies by plan and diagnosis.
