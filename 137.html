How to Get Ozempic: Prescriptions, Costs and Coverage

Getting Ozempic starts with a prescription from a doctor. Here's what happens next.

Step 1: Get a Prescription

Ozempic requires a prescription. You can get one from your primary care doctor, an endocrinologist, or through a telehealth provider. Your doctor will determine if Ozempic is right for you based on your medical history and current health.

Step 2: Check Your Insurance Coverage

Most health insurance plans cover Ozempic for type 2 diabetes. Medicare Part D, Medicaid, and private insurers typically include it on their formularies, though many require prior authorization.

Step 3: Know Your Costs

With insurance, your cost depends on your plan's tier and deductible. Many people pay between 20 to 45 dollars per injection with coverage.

Without insurance, a single Ozempic pen costs around 900 to 1,100 dollars. Manufacturer coupons and patient assistance programs can reduce this to as low as 25 dollars per month if you qualify.

Step 4: Fill Your Prescription

Ozempic is available at most major pharmacies: CVS, Walgreens, Walmart, and independent pharmacies. Some require special ordering due to supply constraints.

Online and Telehealth Options

Telehealth platforms like GoodRx Telehealth, Amazon Pharmacy with a licensed doctor, and specialized prescription services can issue prescriptions. Verify the service is legitimate and works with licensed physicians.

Bottom Line

Getting Ozempic involves a prescription, checking coverage, understanding your costs, and filling it at a pharmacy or through mail order. Planning ahead helps avoid delays.
