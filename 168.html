What actually changes your price at online pharmacies

Your prescription cost is not fixed. The same drug at the same dose changes price based on five concrete factors.

Pharmacy choice

The same medication costs different amounts at different pharmacies. A 30-day supply of a common antibiotic might be $15 at one pharmacy and $45 at another, both legitimate, both in-network. Price varies by:

  • Pharmacy contracts with manufacturers and wholesalers
  • Volume discounts the pharmacy has negotiated
  • Whether they use generic or brand-name suppliers

Insurance plan and tier

Your insurance company assigns drugs to tiers, usually 1 through 5. Tier 1 costs you less (often $5 to $15 copay). Tier 5 costs more (often $50 to $100 or percentage of cost). The same drug can be tier 1 at one insurer and tier 3 at another.

Dosage and quantity

Higher doses sometimes cost less per unit than lower doses, particularly with older generic drugs. A 90-day supply typically costs less per day than a 30-day supply.

Location and state

Prescription taxes and regulations vary by state. Some states tax prescriptions, others do not. Pharmacy licensing fees and operating costs differ by region.

Whether you use a discount or coupon

Manufacturer coupons, GoodRx, SingleCare, and pharmacy loyalty programs can drop your price 20 to 70 percent. These work best on brand-name drugs and less often on generics already priced low.

What to do

Compare prices before you fill. Use your pharmacy's website, GoodRx, or SingleCare to see what you actually pay after insurance and any available discount. Ask your doctor if a generic or lower-tier drug will work instead.
