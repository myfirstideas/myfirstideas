What is sermorelin?

Sermorelin is a synthetic form of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), a natural peptide your body produces to signal the pituitary gland to release growth hormone.

How sermorelin works

Unlike human growth hormone (HGH) replacement therapy, which adds synthetic hormone directly to your system, sermorelin works by prompting your own pituitary gland to produce and release more growth hormone naturally.

Your pituitary responds to the sermorelin signal by releasing growth hormone into your bloodstream in a pattern that mimics your body's natural rhythm.

What sermorelin is used for

Sermorelin is FDA-approved to treat growth hormone deficiency in children. Off-label use in adults centers on:

  • Age-related decline in growth hormone levels
  • Muscle loss and weakness
  • Reduced energy and exercise capacity
  • Body composition changes

How it's administered

Sermorelin is given as a subcutaneous injection, typically once daily, usually in the evening to align with your body's natural growth hormone release pattern.

Sermorelin vs HGH replacement

Sermorelin stimulates your own hormone production. HGH replacement adds synthetic hormone directly. Sermorelin users report a gentler transition and fewer side effects, though response times vary between individuals.

Common side effects

Most users tolerate sermorelin well. Possible effects include injection site reactions, flushing, dizziness, or temporary headache. Serious adverse events are rare.

Getting sermorelin

Sermorelin requires a prescription from a licensed clinician. Many providers now offer sermorelin consultations and prescriptions through telemedicine platforms, making access more convenient than traditional in-office visits.
