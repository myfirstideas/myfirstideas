Zepbound and Mounjaro are the same drug in different regulatory lanes.

Both contain tirzepatide. Both work by mimicking GLP-1 and GIP hormones to reduce appetite and improve blood sugar. The molecule is identical.

What differs is FDA approval and what insurers will cover.

The approval split

Mounjaro arrived first, approved by the FDA in May 2022 for type 2 diabetes. Zepbound followed in November 2023, approved specifically for weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with weight-related conditions.

Same drug. Different labelled uses.

Insurance coverage reality

Mounjaro typically qualifies as a diabetes medication. Most insurance plans, including Medicare Part D in many cases, recognise it for this indication.

Zepbound is approved for weight management. Insurance companies treat weight loss differently from diabetes treatment. Coverage is narrower, prior authorisation is common, and out-of-pocket costs tend to be higher.

What this means for prescribing

If your doctor prescribes Zepbound for weight loss, your insurance may deny or delay coverage. The same prescription written as Mounjaro for diabetes might sail through.

Some patients and providers work around this using off-label prescribing, but that shifts cost and coverage decisions to you.

The practical choice

If you have type 2 diabetes, Mounjaro is the path of least resistance. Insurance recognises it, coverage is more predictable, and your costs are likely lower.

If you're seeking weight loss without diabetes, Zepbound is the approved option, but prepare for tougher insurance conversations and potentially higher expenses.

Both are tirzepatide. The difference is paperwork and money.
