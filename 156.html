Zepbound Prior Authorization: What You Need to Know

Zepbound (tirzepatide) often requires prior authorization before your insurance will cover it. Here's what that means for you.

Why Prior Authorization Is Required

Insurers use prior authorization to verify that a medication meets their coverage criteria. For Zepbound, this typically means checking that you have a documented diagnosis and that you meet the plan's requirements.

What Your Doctor Needs to Submit

Your healthcare provider will submit a prior authorization request that includes:

  • Your medical history and current diagnosis
  • Previous weight loss treatments you've tried
  • Your BMI or weight-related health conditions
  • The specific Zepbound dose being requested

Many insurers have standardized forms available on their websites. Your doctor's office handles the submission.

Timeline and Next Steps

Most insurers decide on prior authorization requests within 24 to 72 hours. If your request is denied, you have the right to appeal.

If You're Denied

Don't assume a denial is final. You can:

  • Ask your doctor to provide additional clinical information
  • File a formal appeal with your insurance company
  • Request an expedited review if medically necessary
  • Ask about your plan's appeals process and timeline

Keep copies of all correspondence with your insurer and your doctor's office throughout the process.

Cost Without Coverage

If prior authorization isn't approved and you don't have coverage, Zepbound costs around $1,350 per month at retail prices. Some manufacturers offer patient assistance programs that may help reduce costs.

Your doctor and insurance company are resources: ask questions about coverage, requirements, and your options.
