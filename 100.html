Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, commonly known as Bactrim, is a combination antibiotic that pairs two separate antibacterial agents. Doctors prescribe it to treat a range of bacterial infections.

What it treats

Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole works against bacterial infections including urinary tract infections, middle ear infections, certain respiratory infections, and some types of pneumonia. It can also treat traveler's diarrhea and certain infections in people with weakened immune systems.

How it works

The medication combines trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole, which work together to block the bacteria's ability to produce folic acid, a chemical essential for bacterial growth and reproduction. This two-pronged approach makes the combination effective against a broader range of bacteria than either drug alone.

Dosing and cost

Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole comes as tablets, suspension, or injection, with dosing adjusted for the specific infection being treated. A standard course typically runs 5 to 14 days. Generic versions are inexpensive and widely available.

Side effects and cautions

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, and rash. Serious allergies can occur, particularly in people with sulfa allergies. The medication interacts with certain blood thinners and some diabetes medications. Pregnant women, especially in the first and third trimesters, should avoid it. Patients with severe kidney or liver problems need dose adjustments.

Always take the full course as prescribed, even if you feel better before finishing.
