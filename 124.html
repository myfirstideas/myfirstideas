Insurance or self-pay for online care: the numbers

When you book a telehealth visit, you have a choice: use insurance or pay out of pocket. The math on which costs less depends on your plan.

Self-pay telehealth costs

Uninsured patients and those with high deductibles often pay $50 to $150 per visit for online care, with follow-ups sometimes cheaper. Prescription fees come separately. No claim processing, no waiting for authorization: you pay at booking and see the clinician the same day or next day.

Insurance-covered telehealth costs

With insurance, your out-of-pocket cost is your copay: usually $15 to $50 per visit for in-network providers. Your plan covers the rest. The catch: authorization delays can push your appointment back days or weeks, and out-of-network providers cost more or aren't covered at all.

When self-pay wins

Pay out of pocket if:

  • Your deductible is high and you haven't met it yet
  • Your plan doesn't cover telehealth or only covers specific conditions
  • You need to see someone out of network
  • Speed matters more than cost

When insurance wins

Use your plan if:

  • Your copay is $25 or less
  • You've already met your deductible
  • Your provider is in-network
  • You're managing a chronic condition with regular visits

The real calculation

Add up your expected visits per year. Multiply by your copay. Then multiply your self-pay rate by the same number of visits. Whichever total is lower is your cheaper option. Don't forget: self-pay visits don't count toward your deductible, but they do add up fast if you need ongoing care.
