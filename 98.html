Tranexamic acid is a medication that reduces bleeding by helping your blood clot more effectively. It works by blocking the breakdown of clots that have already formed, giving them time to stabilise.

What tranexamic acid treats

Doctors prescribe it for heavy menstrual bleeding, nosebleeds that won't stop, bleeding after tooth extraction or surgery, and trauma-related blood loss. It's also used to manage bleeding in people with certain blood disorders.

How to take it

Dosage and route depend on what you're treating. For heavy periods, you might take tablets during your menstrual cycle. For acute bleeding, it can be given intravenously in hospital or emergency settings. Follow your doctor's instructions exactly.

Side effects and cautions

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and headache. More serious risks include blood clots in unusual places, particularly in people with a history of clotting problems. Tell your doctor if you have kidney disease, heart problems or if you're taking other medications.

Don't take tranexamic acid if you have colour blindness from previous use of the drug, or if you're allergic to it.

When to seek help

Contact your doctor if you develop leg pain, chest pain, shortness of breath, vision changes or if bleeding doesn't improve after treatment.
