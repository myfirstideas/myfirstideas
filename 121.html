How to get birth control online without overpaying

Birth control costs vary wildly depending on where you buy it. A month of pills might cost $15 at one pharmacy and $45 at another, even before insurance. Online prescribing and fulfillment adds another layer of pricing variation.

Price variation is real

You can pay different amounts for the same medication depending on:

  • Which telehealth provider writes your prescription
  • Which pharmacy you use to fill it
  • Whether you have insurance and what it covers
  • Manufacturer coupons or patient assistance programs available at that moment

How to find the best price

Get the prescription first. Use a telehealth provider if you don't have an in-person doctor, or ask your existing doctor for a script you can shop around with. Many telehealth services charge $30 to $100 for the visit.

Price the medication at multiple pharmacies. Don't assume the pharmacy that fulfills your telehealth prescription is cheapest. Use GoodRx, SingleCare or RxSaver to check prices at:

  • Major chains (CVS, Walgreens, Rite Aid)
  • Discount chains (Kroger, Walmart)
  • Independent pharmacies
  • Mail order and online pharmacies

Look for manufacturer coupons. Many birth control makers offer savings directly. Ortho, Yaz and others frequently have coupons that stack with pharmacy discounts.

Ask about generics. Most popular birth control pills have generic versions that cost significantly less than brand names.

Insurance considerations

Under the Affordable Care Act, most insurance plans cover at least one birth control method at no cost. Check your plan's formulary before paying out of pocket, especially if you're using a telehealth provider outside your insurance network.
