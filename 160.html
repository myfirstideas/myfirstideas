Medicaid Coverage for Ozempic: What Actually Determines It

Medicaid covers Ozempic (semaglutide) for type 2 diabetes in all 50 states, but what that coverage looks like depends on rules you need to know.

The Coverage Requirements That Matter

Most state Medicaid programs will cover Ozempic only if:

  • You have type 2 diabetes (not off-label use for weight loss)
  • Other diabetes medications haven't worked first
  • Your doctor submits prior authorization
  • You meet your state's specific clinical criteria

Some states require you to try and fail on metformin or GLP-1 agonists first. Others are more flexible. Neither approach is wrong; they're just different gates at different state programs.

What Changes Coverage State to State

Your state's preferred drug list (PDL) determines whether Ozempic sits in a tier that costs you $0, $15, or more. Texas Medicaid might cover it tier 1; your state might tier it higher. Ask your state program directly, not your pharmacy. Pharmacies see their formulary snapshot, not the state's actual coverage rules.

Prior authorization is the gating mechanism in most states. Approved; not approved; conditionally approved if you fail first-line agents. This decision lands with your doctor and the state program, not with you.

What You Need to Do

Start with your state Medicaid office, not your doctor's office. They know your state's rules, tier placement, and prior auth requirements. Your doctor handles the clinical case for why you need it. Both conversations are necessary.
