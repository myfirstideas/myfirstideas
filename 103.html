What is ulipristal?

Ulipristal is a selective progesterone receptor modulator (SPRM) used primarily as emergency contraception. It's sold under the brand name ella in the US and is available by prescription only.

How ulipristal works

Ulipristal works by delaying or inhibiting ovulation, preventing pregnancy after unprotected intercourse. It's most effective when taken as soon as possible after intercourse.

When to use ulipristal

Ulipristal is used for emergency contraception within 120 hours (5 days) of unprotected intercourse or contraceptive failure. It can be used:

  • After unprotected sex
  • If a condom broke
  • If you missed birth control pills
  • After non-consensual intercourse

Effectiveness

Ulipristal is more effective than levonorgestrel (the active ingredient in Plan B) when taken between 72 and 120 hours after intercourse. Earlier use increases effectiveness in both cases.

How to take ulipristal

Ulipristal comes as a single 30 mg tablet taken by mouth. You don't need to time it with meals.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, nausea, abdominal pain, fatigue, and dizziness. Most side effects are mild and temporary.

Important considerations

Ulipristal requires a prescription and is not the same as the abortion pill. It prevents pregnancy; it does not end an existing pregnancy. It's less effective if you're already pregnant.

Access and cost

Availability varies by state and insurance coverage. Telehealth providers can prescribe ulipristal in many locations, offering a private and convenient option for obtaining emergency contraception.
