What is minoxidil?

Minoxidil is a topical medication that treats androgenetic alopecia, the most common form of hair loss in both men and women. It comes as a liquid, foam or spray that you apply directly to your scalp.

Originally developed as an oral medication for high blood pressure, minoxidil was discovered to regrow hair as a side effect. The topical version has been used for hair loss since the 1980s and remains one of the few over-the-counter treatments with solid clinical evidence behind it.

How minoxidil works

Minoxidil works by widening blood vessels around hair follicles. This increases blood flow and nutrient delivery to follicles, potentially moving them from a resting phase back into an active growth phase. The exact mechanism isn't completely understood, but the effect on hair regrowth is well documented in clinical trials.

Realistic expectations

Minoxidil doesn't work for everyone. In clinical trials, about 40 percent of people using minoxidil experienced moderate hair regrowth. Another 40 percent saw some regrowth. The remaining 20 percent saw little to no improvement.

You'll need to use minoxidil continuously. Hair regrowth typically takes 3 to 6 months to become noticeable. If you stop using it, any regrowth will be lost within weeks or months.

Common side effects

The most frequent side effect is scalp irritation, including itching, redness and dryness. Some people experience unwanted facial hair growth from minoxidil that runs down the face. Headaches and dizziness can occur but are less common with topical versions. If you experience chest pain, rapid heartbeat or shortness of breath, stop using minoxidil and seek medical attention.

Cost and access

Minoxidil is available over the counter without a prescription at most pharmacies. Generic versions cost less than brand names like Rogaine. Expect to spend 20 to 40 dollars per month depending on the formulation and brand. Insurance typically doesn't cover minoxidil since it's an over-the-counter medication.

Minoxidil vs other treatments

Finasteride (Propecia) works differently by blocking the hormone responsible for pattern hair loss rather than improving blood flow. Many people use both medications together for better results. Newer prescription options like dutasteride target the same pathway as finasteride but may be more effective for some people.

Telehealth providers can prescribe minoxidil, finasteride or other hair loss treatments online after evaluating your medical history and scalp.

Bottom line

Minoxidil is an affordable, accessible first step for pattern hair loss. It works for about 80 percent of people to some degree, but results vary. You need realistic expectations about timeline and consistency: use it daily for at least 3 to 6 months before deciding if it works for you.
