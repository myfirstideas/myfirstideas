What is Ipamorelin?

Ipamorelin is a synthetic peptide that works as a growth hormone secretagogue. It stimulates your pituitary gland to release growth hormone naturally, rather than delivering it directly. This makes it different from straight human growth hormone (HGH) injections.

The drug operates by mimicking ghrelin, a hormone that signals hunger and growth hormone release. When you inject ipamorelin, it binds to ghrelin receptors and tells your body to produce more of its own growth hormone.

How It's Used

Ipamorelin is administered by subcutaneous injection, typically once daily or as directed by a prescriber. The peptide is not approved by the FDA for any medical condition, but it's prescribed off-label in anti-aging clinics and by some telehealth providers specializing in hormone optimization.

Common claimed uses include:

  • Increasing lean muscle mass
  • Reducing body fat
  • Improving skin elasticity and wound healing
  • Enhancing athletic performance
  • Supporting metabolic function

What the Evidence Says

Ipamorelin has shown activity in clinical research, primarily European studies. It stimulates growth hormone release in a dose-dependent way. However, long-term safety data in humans is limited, and most evidence comes from short-duration trials.

No major medical organizations recommend ipamorelin for anti-aging or performance enhancement. The American Academy of Anti-Aging Medicine acknowledges peptide research but does not endorse routine clinical use.

Side Effects and Safety Concerns

Common reported side effects include:

  • Injection site reactions (redness, soreness)
  • Headache
  • Flushing
  • Dizziness
  • Nausea

Serious risks remain understudied because ipamorelin use is off-label and largely unregulated. Potential concerns based on growth hormone biology include:

  • Long-term effects on insulin sensitivity
  • Impact on cancer risk (theoretical, not proven)
  • Joint and nerve pain
  • Carpal tunnel symptoms

People with a personal or family history of cancer should avoid ipamorelin until safety is clearer.

Cost and Insurance

Ipamorelin is not covered by insurance because it's not FDA-approved for any indication. Out-of-pocket costs typically range from $200 to $400 per month at specialized clinics, depending on dosage and provider.

Who Should Not Use It

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding people
  • Anyone with active cancer or cancer history
  • People with pituitary gland disorders
  • Those taking certain diabetes medications
  • Anyone under 18 (growth plates may still be open)

Bottom Line

Ipamorelin works as a growth hormone secretagogue in research settings. But it's not approved, long-term safety is unclear, and efficacy for anti-aging claims remains modest in peer review. If you're considering it, consult a doctor who can review your full medical history and monitor hormone levels and metabolic markers over time.
