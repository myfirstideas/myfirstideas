Wegovy has regulatory approval and clinical evidence behind it, but safety depends on honest answers about your health history and close monitoring during treatment.

What the trials showed

The clinical studies that led to FDA approval tracked semaglutide's effects in people with obesity over 68 weeks. Weight loss was significant, but so were the side effects. Most were gastrointestinal: nausea, vomiting, constipation, and diarrhoea. They often improved over time as bodies adjusted to the medication.

More serious but rare events occurred at low rates: pancreatitis, gallbladder issues, and kidney problems. The trials found no unexpected safety signals in cardiovascular outcomes for most patients.

Who needs to be careful

Wegovy is not safe for everyone. It carries absolute contraindications:

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer
  • Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2
  • Pregnancy (it causes fetal harm)

It requires caution in people with:

  • A history of pancreatitis
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Severe kidney impairment
  • Diabetic retinopathy (in some cases)

Real-world safety data

Post-market monitoring has not revealed unexpected serious adverse events at scale, but millions of people now use semaglutide. Common reasons for stopping include ongoing nausea, cost, or loss of appetite becoming problematic rather than helpful.

The honest bottom line

Wegovy is a medication with evidence of efficacy and an established safety profile when used in people it was designed for. That does not mean it is safe for you without proper screening, dosing, and follow-up. Your doctor needs your complete medical history and should monitor you during treatment.
