Spironolactone is a potassium-sparing diuretic that blocks aldosterone, a hormone that controls salt and water balance in your body. Unlike other diuretics, it doesn't make you lose potassium, which is why doctors often use it alongside other blood pressure medications.

What spironolactone treats

Doctors prescribe spironolactone for high blood pressure, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and hormonal acne driven by excess androgens. It's particularly useful when potassium loss is a concern with other diuretics.

How it works

Spironolactone blocks aldosterone receptors in your kidneys. This reduces sodium reabsorption and water retention, lowering blood pressure. The same mechanism that preserves potassium makes it valuable in heart failure treatment, where potassium depletion can trigger dangerous heart rhythms.

Side effects and considerations

Common side effects include dizziness, headache and nausea. More serious concerns are high potassium levels (hyperkalemia), particularly in people with kidney disease or those taking ACE inhibitors. Men may experience breast tenderness or enlarged breast tissue. Women often see improved acne and reduced facial hair growth.

Who should avoid it

Spironolactone isn't suitable if you have severe kidney disease, high potassium levels or take medications that raise potassium. Talk to your doctor about all medications and supplements before starting.

Your doctor will monitor potassium and kidney function with blood tests during treatment.
