Phentermine is a prescription appetite suppressant in the stimulant class. It's one of the oldest weight-loss medications still in regular use, approved by the FDA in 1959.

How phentermine works

Phentermine stimulates the release of norepinephrine in your central nervous system. This triggers your brain to feel less hungry and gives you more energy. The effect is real but temporary: your body adapts over time, which is why it's meant for short-term use only.

Typical dosing and duration

Phentermine comes in immediate-release and extended-release forms. Doses range from 15 mg to 37.5 mg daily, taken in the morning. Treatment typically lasts 12 weeks or less, though some doctors prescribe it intermittently for longer periods.

What the research shows

Combined with diet and exercise, phentermine can produce modest weight loss: studies show average losses of 3 to 5 kg (roughly 7 to 11 pounds) beyond what diet and exercise alone achieve over 12 weeks. Results vary widely between individuals.

Side effects and contraindications

Common side effects include dry mouth, insomnia, headache and increased heart rate. Phentermine raises blood pressure and isn't suitable for people with uncontrolled hypertension, heart disease or certain psychiatric conditions. It can interact with other medications, particularly SSRIs and other stimulants.

Getting phentermine

Phentermine requires a prescription and in-person evaluation. Many telehealth providers now offer phentermine consultations, though some states restrict remote prescribing of controlled substances. If approved, your doctor will monitor your progress and vital signs during treatment.
