Choosing a TRT Urologist: What Matters

Low testosterone treatment requires a doctor who understands both the condition and you. Here's how to find one.

Credentials That Count

Look for board certification in urology from the American Board of Urology. This means the doctor has completed approved training and passed rigorous exams. Fellowship training in andrology (male reproductive health) is a strong signal of deeper expertise in hormone treatment.

Questions to Ask

Ask how many testosterone patients they treat monthly. Doctors who regularly prescribe TRT understand the nuances: which delivery methods work for which patients, how to monitor safely, and when adjustments help.

Ask about their testing protocol. Legitimate TRT starts with blood work showing genuinely low testosterone, not just a symptom checklist. A good doctor tests more than once and checks related hormones.

Ask what happens after you start treatment. TRT requires follow-up blood work, usually at 6-8 weeks, then annually. Doctors who skip this are cutting corners.

Red Flags

Stay away from doctors who:

  • Diagnose low testosterone from symptoms alone
  • Offer only one delivery method
  • Promise quick fixes or dramatic life changes
  • Don't discuss potential side effects
  • Charge cash-only and skip insurance verification

Location and Access

Check whether the practice offers telehealth appointments for follow-ups. Many states allow hormone management through telemedicine after an initial in-person visit, which can save time and travel.

Finding Candidates

Start with your primary care doctor's referrals. Check your insurance plan's provider directory for in-network urologists. Read patient reviews that mention specific experience with TRT, not just general satisfaction. Ask local men's health clinics for recommendations.

The Bottom Line

The right TRT urologist listens, tests thoroughly, explains options clearly, and monitors your care. Trust takes time to build, so interview more than one if you can.
