What is Fluoxetine?

Fluoxetine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, commonly known as an SSRI. It's one of the most prescribed antidepressants in the US and has been in use since the 1980s.

What It Treats

Doctors prescribe fluoxetine for:

  • Depression
  • Generalized anxiety disorder
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)
  • Panic disorder
  • Bulimia nervosa
  • Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD)
  • Some off-label uses for other anxiety and mood conditions

How It Works

Fluoxetine increases serotonin levels in the brain by blocking its reabsorption. This helps regulate mood, reduce anxiety, and ease obsessive thoughts and compulsive behaviors.

Common Side Effects

Many people tolerate fluoxetine well. Common side effects include:

  • Nausea (often improves after a few weeks)
  • Sleep disruption or drowsiness
  • Sexual dysfunction
  • Headache
  • Dry mouth
  • Loss of appetite

Side effects often decrease as your body adjusts to the medication.

Dosing and Timing

Fluoxetine typically starts at 20 mg daily. Doctors may adjust dosage based on your response and tolerance. It takes 4 to 6 weeks to feel full effects.

Important Considerations

Don't stop taking fluoxetine abruptly without talking to your doctor. Withdrawal symptoms can occur. Tell your doctor about all medications and supplements you take, as fluoxetine interacts with some drugs.
