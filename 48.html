What is Estradiol?

Estradiol is a form of estrogen, a hormone naturally produced in the body. It's prescribed to replace estrogen when levels drop or to treat specific conditions where estrogen therapy is needed.

Common Uses

Estradiol treats:

  • Menopause symptoms like hot flashes and night sweats
  • Vaginal dryness and discomfort
  • Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)
  • Certain breast and prostate cancers
  • Low estrogen levels from other medical conditions

How It's Delivered

Estradiol comes in multiple forms:

  • Tablets
  • Patches
  • Creams and gels
  • Injections
  • Vaginal rings

Your doctor will choose the form based on your condition and needs.

What to Know Before Taking It

Estradiol isn't suitable for everyone. Tell your doctor if you have:

  • A history of blood clots
  • Breast cancer or family history of it
  • Liver disease
  • High blood pressure
  • Heart disease

Side Effects

Common side effects include:

  • Nausea or stomach upset
  • Breast tenderness
  • Headaches
  • Mood changes
  • Bloating

Most side effects improve within weeks. Serious side effects are rare but require immediate medical attention.

Important Considerations

Hormone therapy decisions are individual. Discuss risks and benefits with your doctor, especially if you're over 60 or have other health conditions. Regular monitoring helps ensure the medication is working safely for you.
