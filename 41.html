What is DHEA?

DHEA (dehydroepiandrosterone) is a hormone your body produces naturally in the adrenal glands. Your levels peak in your 20s and 30s, then gradually decline with age.

As a supplement, DHEA is available over the counter and marketed for everything from energy and mood to muscle and sexual function. The pitch is straightforward: restore what age takes away.

How DHEA works

Your body converts DHEA into estrogen and testosterone. The idea behind supplementation is that topping up your DHEA restores those downstream hormones to younger levels, which might improve strength, mood, bone density, and sexual function.

What actually happens is more complicated.

What the evidence shows

Research on DHEA is mixed and often modest in scope. Some studies show small improvements in sexual function and mood in older adults, particularly people with low baseline DHEA levels. Others show little effect.

Evidence for muscle gain, weight loss, or cognitive benefits is weak. Most studies that do show benefit involve small sample sizes or short time periods.

Who might consider it

DHEA makes the most sense for older adults with documented low DHEA levels who are experiencing fatigue, low mood, or sexual dysfunction. A doctor can check your levels.

It's less clear whether younger people with normal DHEA levels benefit from supplementing.

What to watch for

DHEA is not risk-free. It can raise estrogen and testosterone levels unpredictably, which matters if you have a history of hormone-sensitive cancer, heart disease, or liver problems. It may also interact with medications or affect blood sugar.

Because supplements are not FDA-regulated like medications, quality and purity vary by brand.

The bottom line

DHEA is a legitimate hormone, and the supplement is legal. But it's not a fix-all. If you're considering it, get your DHEA levels tested first, talk to your doctor about whether it makes sense for your situation, and start with a low dose to see how you respond.
