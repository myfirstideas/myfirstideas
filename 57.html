What insulin lispro does

Insulin lispro is a rapid-acting insulin. It starts working within 15 minutes of injection, peaks at around 1 hour, and keeps working for 2 to 4 hours.

It's designed to cover the blood sugar spike that happens when you eat. Most people inject it at the start of a meal.

When it's prescribed

Your doctor may prescribe insulin lispro if:

  • You have type 1 diabetes and need mealtime insulin coverage
  • You have type 2 diabetes and other medications aren't controlling blood sugar well enough
  • You need quick insulin action at specific times of day

How to use it

Insulin lispro comes as:

  • Prefilled pens (KwikPen)
  • Vials you draw from with a syringe
  • Insulin pumps

You inject it under the skin. Your doctor or diabetes educator will show you how to inject safely and rotate injection sites to avoid skin damage.

What to watch for

Like all insulins, lispro can cause low blood sugar (hypoglycemia). Symptoms include shakiness, sweating, confusion, and dizziness. Carry fast-acting sugar (juice, glucose tablets) if you use this insulin.

Other side effects may include skin reactions at injection sites or allergic reactions, though these are uncommon.

Cost and access

Insulin lispro is on most insurance formularies. Brand name Humalog and generic versions are available. Your out-of-pocket cost depends on your insurance plan and whether you choose brand or generic.
