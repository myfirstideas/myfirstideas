What is Progesterone?

Progesterone is a hormone medication that replaces or supplements the progesterone your body naturally produces. Your ovaries make progesterone after ovulation each month, and it plays a key role in regulating your menstrual cycle and preparing your body for pregnancy.

How Progesterone Works

Progesterone works by binding to receptors in your uterus, brain, and breast tissue. It thickens the uterine lining, which can prevent pregnancy if you're taking it as a contraceptive, or support pregnancy if you're trying to conceive.

Common Uses

Doctors prescribe progesterone for several reasons:

  • Regulating irregular or absent periods
  • Supporting early pregnancy in people at risk of miscarriage
  • Treating symptoms of low progesterone
  • Managing heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
  • Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) during perimenopause and menopause
  • Contraception (combined with estrogen or as a standalone option)

How You Take It

Progesterone comes in multiple forms: oral pills, vaginal inserts, injections, and skin creams. Your doctor will recommend the form and dose based on why you need it and your medical history.

Side Effects

Common side effects include headache, breast tenderness, mood changes, and bloating. Most people tolerate progesterone well, but some experience nausea or dizziness.
