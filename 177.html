Timeline: When Ozempic Starts Working

Ozempic (semaglutide) works in stages. Most people notice appetite suppression within 3 to 5 days of their first injection. Blood sugar control improves faster than weight loss.

Week 1 to 2

You may feel less hungry or fuller faster during meals. This is the GLP-1 receptor working on your appetite centers. Some people report nausea in these early days.

Week 2 to 4

Weight loss typically begins by week 2, though the amount varies by person. Blood sugar levels start dropping more noticeably if you have type 2 diabetes.

Month 2 to 3

Steady weight loss continues as your body adjusts. Most people reach a clearer sense of their individual response by week 8 to 12.

Why the Lag Between Appetite and Weight Loss?

Feeling less hungry is immediate. Losing pounds takes time because your body is still burning through existing calories and adjusting to lower food intake. Consistent results appear around week 4.

Dose Escalation Matters

Ozempic starts at 0.25 mg once weekly, then increases every 4 weeks (0.5 mg, then 1 mg, then up to 2 mg). Full appetite suppression effects often come at higher doses. If you're on the starter dose, wait for the escalation schedule before deciding if it's working.

What to Expect: Realistic Targets

Average weight loss is 5% to 15% of body weight over 6 months on Ozempic. Some people lose more; some lose less. Blood sugar control improves in most type 2 diabetes cases within the first month.
