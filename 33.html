Azelaic acid is a naturally occurring dicarboxylic acid found in grains like barley, wheat, and rye. It comes in both over-the-counter and prescription strengths, making it accessible for a range of skin conditions.

What azelaic acid treats

Azelaic acid is FDA-approved for rosacea, where it reduces redness and inflammatory bumps. Dermatologists also prescribe and recommend it for acne, particularly when bacteria-resistant strains are involved, and for post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (dark marks left after acne or other skin injury heals).

How it works

Azelaic acid works through multiple mechanisms. It has antibacterial properties that target acne-causing bacteria. It also reduces inflammation and normalises skin cell turnover, which helps prevent clogged pores. For pigmentation issues, it inhibits melanin production without the irritation some people experience from other brightening agents.

Strength and formulation

Over-the-counter azelaic acid typically comes in 10 to 20 percent concentrations. Prescription versions reach up to 20 percent and are often formulated for better penetration and faster results. Common formulations include creams, gels, and foams, so you can choose based on your skin type and preference.

What to expect

Results usually take 4 to 12 weeks of consistent use. Some people experience mild irritation, redness, or dryness when starting, particularly with prescription strengths. Beginning with a lower concentration and using it a few times a week can help your skin adjust.

Azelaic acid works well alongside other treatments and is generally safe for all skin tones, though those with sensitive skin should introduce it gradually.
