When virtual visits work: telehealth vs in-person care

Not every health issue needs an in-person appointment. Virtual visits work well for some conditions and situations, while others require a physical exam.

What telehealth handles well

Virtual visits are effective for:

  • Follow-up appointments on stable conditions
  • Prescription refills and medication reviews
  • Mild skin issues you can show on camera
  • Respiratory infections like colds or flu
  • Urinary tract infections (with symptom assessment)
  • Mental health and counselling
  • Routine check-ins after surgery or treatment
  • Medication side effect discussions

These situations don't typically need hands-on examination. Your doctor can diagnose and treat based on your description, symptoms and medical history.

When you need in-person care

Some health problems require physical examination:

  • Chest pain or difficulty breathing
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Suspected broken bones or severe injuries
  • Conditions requiring blood pressure checks or blood work
  • Lumps, bumps or skin lesions that need touch assessment
  • Joint injuries requiring range-of-motion testing
  • Ear, nose or throat problems needing otoscope examination
  • Potential infections needing culture samples

The practical middle ground

Many patients use both approaches. You might have a telehealth visit first for assessment, then go in-person if your doctor needs to examine you. Some practices offer hybrid care this way.

The key is knowing your condition. If you're unsure whether virtual makes sense, call your provider and ask. They know what they can and can't diagnose through a screen.
