What is bremelanotide?

Bremelanotide is a medication approved by the FDA to treat hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women. It's the first and only non-hormonal treatment for this condition.

How bremelanotide works

Bremelanotide activates melanocortin receptors in the brain. These receptors regulate sexual desire. The medication comes as a pre-filled auto-injector pen that you inject under the skin before anticipated sexual activity.

Who can use it

Bremelanotide is approved only for premenopausal women with diagnosed HSDD. It's not suitable for:

  • Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • People with uncontrolled high blood pressure
  • Those taking certain blood pressure medications
  • People with a history of skin cancer or concerning skin lesions

How to use bremelanotide

You inject the medication 45 minutes to 3 hours before sexual activity. The dose is 0.72 mg injected under the skin of your abdomen or thigh. You should not use it more than once per 24 hours or more than 8 times per month.

Side effects

Common side effects include:

  • Nausea
  • Facial flushing
  • Headache
  • Injection site reactions
  • Darkening of moles or freckles

Side effects are usually mild and improve over time.

Cost and access

Bremelanotide is a prescription medication typically costing hundreds of dollars per month without insurance. Coverage varies significantly by insurance plan. Your doctor can help you navigate access options and check coverage with your plan.
