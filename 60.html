What is Letrozole?

Letrozole is an aromatase inhibitor, a class of medication that blocks the enzyme aromatase, which converts other hormones into estrogen in postmenopausal women. It's used for two main purposes: treating hormone receptor-positive breast cancer and stimulating ovulation in fertility treatment.

How Letrozole Works

By inhibiting aromatase, letrozole reduces estrogen levels in the body. In breast cancer treatment, lower estrogen slows or stops the growth of hormone-dependent cancer cells. In fertility treatment, the medication stimulates the pituitary gland to release hormones that trigger ovulation.

Primary Uses

Breast Cancer Treatment: Letrozole is prescribed for postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive breast cancer, either as initial therapy or after tamoxifen treatment.

Fertility Treatment: Doctors prescribe letrozole off-label to stimulate ovulation in women with infertility, often as an alternative to clomiphene citrate.

Side Effects and Considerations

Common side effects include hot flashes, joint pain, fatigue, and headache. More serious but less common effects may include bone loss and cardiovascular concerns. Individual responses vary significantly.

Cost and Access

Letrozole is available as a generic medication, making it more affordable than brand-name alternatives. Most insurance plans cover it for approved indications, though coverage policies vary. Out-of-pocket costs depend on your specific plan and pharmacy.

Getting Started

Letrozole requires a prescription and regular monitoring by your healthcare provider. Discuss your medical history, current medications, and treatment goals before starting.
