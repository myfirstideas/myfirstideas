Zepbound Insurance Coverage: What's Covered and Your Options If It's Not

Zepbound (tirzepatide) costs around $1,300 per month without insurance. Most plans won't cover it automatically, so understanding your coverage options matters.

Which Insurance Plans Cover Zepbound

Coverage varies by plan type and insurer. Medicare covers Zepbound only for people with type 2 diabetes plus heart or kidney disease. Medicaid coverage depends on your state. Many commercial plans cover it for weight loss, but require prior authorization and specific criteria.

What Insurers Require

Most plans that cover Zepbound ask for:

  • A BMI of 30 or higher (or 27+ with weight-related conditions)
  • Documented weight loss attempts with other methods
  • A diagnosis of type 2 diabetes or obesity
  • Prior authorization from your doctor

Some plans limit coverage to a certain number of pens per year or require you to try generic alternatives first.

What To Do If Your Insurance Won't Cover It

If your plan denies coverage:

  1. Ask your doctor to submit a medical necessity appeal
  2. Check if your state Medicaid program covers it differently
  3. Look into manufacturer assistance programs (Eli Lilly offers copay cards)
  4. Compare costs at different pharmacies using GoodRx or similar tools
  5. Ask about cash-pay discounts directly from your pharmacy

Some people find that switching plans during open enrollment helps, though this takes planning.

The Cost Difference

With insurance, copays typically run $35 to $250 per month. Without coverage, cash prices range from $700 to $1,400 depending on pharmacy and program discounts. Assistance programs can bring this down significantly.
