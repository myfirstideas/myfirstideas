What is escitalopram?

Escitalopram is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, or SSRI. It's one of the most commonly prescribed antidepressants in the United States.

What does it treat?

Escitalopram is FDA-approved for:

  • Major depressive disorder
  • Generalized anxiety disorder

Doctors may also prescribe it off-label for panic disorder, social anxiety, OCD, and PTSD.

How it works

SSRIs like escitalopram work by increasing serotonin levels in your brain. Serotonin is a neurotransmitter that affects mood, sleep, and anxiety. By blocking the reabsorption of serotonin, escitalopram allows more of it to stay active in your system.

What to expect

Most people notice improvements in mood and anxiety within 2 to 4 weeks, though full effects can take 6 to 8 weeks. It usually takes some trial and adjustment to find your right dose.

Typical side effects

  • Nausea (usually improves within a few days)
  • Headache
  • Sleep changes
  • Sexual side effects (in some patients)
  • Weight changes
  • Dry mouth

Most side effects are mild and temporary. Talk to your doctor if they persist or affect your quality of life.

Key questions for your doctor

  • How long until I should expect to feel better?
  • What's the plan if this doesn't work or side effects are difficult?
  • Are there any interactions with other medications I take?
  • How long should I stay on this medication?

Important notes

Don't stop escitalopram abruptly. Stopping suddenly can cause withdrawal symptoms. Work with your doctor on a tapering plan if you decide to discontinue.
