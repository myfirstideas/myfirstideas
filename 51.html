Fluconazole is an antifungal medication that stops fungi from growing. It treats infections caused by candida, including oral thrush and vaginal yeast infections, as well as other fungal infections like ringworm and athlete's foot.

How It Works

Fluconazole works by disrupting the cell membranes of fungi, preventing them from multiplying and spreading. This gives your immune system time to clear the infection.

Common Uses

Doctors prescribe fluconazole for:

  • Vaginal yeast infections
  • Oral thrush (mouth yeast infection)
  • Esophageal candidiasis
  • Ringworm and other skin fungal infections
  • Fungal nail infections
  • Cryptococcal meningitis (in hospitalized patients)
  • Prevention of fungal infections in people with weakened immune systems

Typical Dosing

Dosing depends on the type and severity of infection. A single 150 mg dose treats most vaginal yeast infections. Oral thrush typically requires 200 mg on day one, then 100 mg daily for 2 weeks. Your doctor will adjust based on your condition and kidney function.

Side Effects to Know

Common side effects include headache, nausea, and abdominal pain. Most are mild and temporary. Serious side effects are rare but can include liver problems and severe skin reactions. Tell your doctor immediately if you develop a rash, yellowing skin, or dark urine.

Before Taking Fluconazole

Tell your doctor if you have liver disease, heart rhythm problems, or take other medications. Fluconazole interacts with certain drugs including blood thinners and some heart medications. Pregnancy requires careful consideration: your doctor will weigh risks and benefits.

Getting Fluconazole

A prescription is required for most formulations. Telehealth providers can diagnose fungal infections and prescribe fluconazole if appropriate for your situation. Many insurance plans cover it, and generic versions are affordable.
