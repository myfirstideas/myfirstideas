Semaglutide comes in two forms: compounded and brand-name. Both contain the same active ingredient, but they differ significantly in cost, regulation and proven safety.

Brand semaglutide: FDA-approved, higher cost

Ozempic (diabetes) and Wegovy (weight loss) are made by Novo Nordisk under strict FDA oversight. You pay more: typically $900 to $1,400 per month for Wegovy without insurance. But you get decades of clinical trial data, consistent dosing, and guaranteed potency.

Compounded semaglutide: cheaper, less regulated

Compounding pharmacies mix semaglutide from raw ingredients. Cost drops to $200 to $400 per month. The catch: compounded drugs skip FDA approval. Quality control varies between pharmacies. Potency can be inconsistent. Long-term safety data doesn't exist yet.

How to decide

Choose brand semaglutide if:

  • You have good insurance coverage
  • You want clinical evidence of safety and efficacy
  • You prefer consistency batch to batch

Choose compounded semaglutide if:

  • Cost is the deciding factor
  • Your insurance denies brand coverage
  • You're willing to accept less regulatory oversight

Ask your prescriber which compounding pharmacy they trust. Check whether they test potency and follow USP standards. Get it in writing.
