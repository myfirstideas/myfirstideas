Rizatriptan for migraines: how it works and what to expect

Rizatriptan is a triptan medication designed to stop migraines once they start. It works by narrowing blood vessels around the brain and blocking pain pathways in the nervous system.

How rizatriptan works

Triptans like rizatriptan activate serotonin receptors in the brain. This constricts blood vessels that have dilated during a migraine and stops the release of substances in the nervous system that cause headache, nausea, sensitivity to light and sound, and other migraine symptoms.

Rizatriptan is not preventive. It treats active migraines, ideally taken as soon as symptoms start.

Dosing and timing

Rizatriptan comes as a 5mg or 10mg tablet. Most people take 10mg at migraine onset. If symptoms return, you can take a second dose after 2 hours, up to 30mg per day.

Taking it early, when the migraine is mild, gives the best results. Waiting until pain is severe reduces effectiveness.

Cost and insurance

Out-of-pocket costs typically range from $20 to $300 per dose, depending on your insurance plan and pharmacy. Generic rizatriptan costs less than brand-name Maxalt. Many insurance plans require prior authorisation or generic substitution.

Side effects

Common side effects include dizziness, tingling, warm sensations and fatigue. Serious side effects are rare but can include chest pain or pressure, high blood pressure and irregular heartbeat.

Rizatriptan is not suitable for people with heart disease, uncontrolled high blood pressure or certain other conditions.

Telemedicine access

You can obtain rizatriptan through telehealth visits with licensed doctors who can diagnose migraines and write prescriptions. Telemedicine appointments typically cost $50 to $150.
