Telehealth subscriptions market themselves as straightforward. One price. One monthly charge. Reality is messier.

The advertised fee covers basic access. What it doesn't always cover: prescription fulfillment, specialist referrals, lab work, insurance coordination, expedited responses. Each one adds a separate charge.

Most platforms charge $50 to $200 per month for the subscription itself. Then the extras:

  • Prescription handling: $0 to $25 per script
  • Specialist consultation: $50 to $150 on top of your subscription
  • Rush appointments: $20 to $40 extra
  • Lab work orders: variable, usually $15 to $50
  • Prescription delivery by courier: $10 to $30

Automatic renewal is standard. You're charged monthly unless you manually cancel. Some platforms make cancellation easy. Others require phone calls or multiple steps.

Read the terms before you subscribe. Check what's actually included in the stated fee versus what costs extra. Ask whether the platform charges for messaging between visits or only for actual consultations. Some do one, some do both.

Compare the subscription cost against pay-per-visit alternatives. If you use the service once or twice monthly, you might pay less without a subscription.

Check your credit card statement. Subscription charges don't always show the company name clearly, making them hard to spot if something unexpected appears.
