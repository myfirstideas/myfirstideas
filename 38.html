Bupropion is an antidepressant medication that treats depression and seasonal affective disorder. Unlike most antidepressants, it works by increasing dopamine and norepinephrine rather than serotonin.

How bupropion works

Bupropion is a norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor (NDRI). This means it prevents your brain from reabsorbing dopamine and norepinephrine, keeping these chemicals available longer. The result is improved mood and motivation.

Common side effects

Many people tolerate bupropion well, but side effects can include:

  • Dry mouth
  • Insomnia or sleep problems
  • Nausea
  • Headache
  • Anxiety or restlessness
  • Loss of appetite

Side effects often improve within the first few weeks as your body adjusts.

Who should take bupropion

Bupropion works well for depression with low motivation or energy. It's also prescribed for seasonal affective disorder and to help people quit smoking. Some people prefer it to SSRIs because it doesn't typically cause weight gain or sexual side effects.

Dosing and timing

Bupropion comes in immediate-release, sustained-release, and extended-release formulations. Dosing depends on which form you take and your individual needs. Your doctor will start with a lower dose and adjust upward as needed.
