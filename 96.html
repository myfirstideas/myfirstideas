Tirzepatide is a prescription medication that works as both a GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonist. This dual action makes it different from older diabetes medications and single-action GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide.

How tirzepatide works

Tirzepatide activates two hormone receptors in your body: GLP-1 and GIP. These receptors help regulate blood sugar, reduce appetite, and slow stomach emptying. The combination approach means it can be more effective at lowering A1C levels and supporting weight loss than medications that target only one receptor.

What tirzepatide is used for

Tirzepatide is approved for type 2 diabetes management. It's also used off-label for weight loss, though the weight loss indication is pending FDA review.

How you take it

Tirzepatide is a once-weekly injection you give yourself. You start at a lower dose and gradually increase it based on how your body responds and your blood sugar goals.

Side effects and tolerability

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation, especially when starting or increasing doses. Most people find these improve over time. Serious side effects are rare but include pancreatitis and gallbladder issues.

Cost and access

Tirzepatide can be expensive without insurance. If cost is a barrier, talk to your doctor about patient assistance programs or ask your pharmacy about generic options as they become available.
