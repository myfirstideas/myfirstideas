Peptide therapy and human growth hormone (HGH) are not the same thing, though they are sometimes confused. Both can affect growth and body composition, but they work through different mechanisms, carry different costs, and operate under different legal frameworks.

How They Work

HGH is a single hormone produced by your pituitary gland. When prescribed medically, it replaces or supplements your body's own production.

Peptide therapy uses short chains of amino acids to trigger specific biological responses. Some peptides stimulate your body to produce more of its own growth hormone. Others work on different pathways entirely.

Cost Differences

HGH prescriptions typically run between 600 and 3,000 USD per month depending on dosage and brand. Peptide therapy costs vary widely: some peptides cost 200 to 500 USD per month, while others exceed 1,000 USD.

Neither is usually covered by insurance when prescribed for anti-ageing or performance purposes.

Legal Status

HGH is a controlled substance. It's legal to prescribe for specific medical conditions: growth hormone deficiency, short stature in children, HIV-related wasting, and a handful of others. Prescribing it for off-label uses like anti-ageing is illegal in the United States.

Peptide therapy operates in a greyer area. Some peptides have approved medical uses. Others are sold as research chemicals or for investigational use only, which means they cannot legally be marketed for human consumption in the US without FDA approval.
