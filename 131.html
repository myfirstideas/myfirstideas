What Telehealth Doctors Can and Cannot Prescribe

Telehealth removes the commute, but it doesn't remove the prescription rules. Online doctors operate under the same legal framework as in-person physicians, which means some medications are available through a screen and others are not.

What telehealth doctors can prescribe

Most routine medications fall into this category. You can get prescriptions for:

  • Antibiotics for infections
  • Blood pressure and cholesterol medications
  • Antidepressants and anti-anxiety drugs
  • Allergy and cold medications
  • Thyroid medications
  • Diabetes drugs
  • Asthma inhalers
  • Pain relievers and anti-inflammatories
  • Hormonal birth control

These medications don't require in-person monitoring that telehealth cannot provide, and they carry a lower risk of misuse.

What telehealth doctors cannot prescribe

Federal regulations and state laws restrict prescribing of certain medications online:

  • Schedule II controlled substances (oxycodone, morphine, Adderall, Ritalin) cannot be prescribed via telemedicine in most cases
  • Benzodiazepines (Xanax, Valium, Ativan) have strict limitations under DEA rules
  • Barbiturates face similar restrictions
  • Injectable medications often require in-person administration
  • Medications requiring blood work monitoring may need initial in-person visits
  • Stimulants typically cannot be prescribed without established care

The fine print

State regulations vary. Some states allow limited telemedicine prescribing of controlled substances under specific conditions, like established patient relationships. Others don't allow it at all.

DEA rules on telehealth changed during the pandemic and some flexibility remains, but prescribers must follow both federal and state law, whichever is stricter.

Before your appointment, check whether your state and chosen provider allow prescribing for what you actually need.
