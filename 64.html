Metformin is a medication that lowers blood sugar levels in people with type 2 diabetes. It's the most commonly prescribed diabetes drug in the US.

How metformin works

Metformin reduces the amount of glucose your liver produces and helps your body use insulin more effectively. It does not increase insulin production.

Common side effects

Gastrointestinal issues are most common, especially when starting:

  • Nausea
  • Diarrhea
  • Stomach upset
  • Metallic taste

These often improve after a few weeks. Taking metformin with food can help reduce stomach problems.

Cost and access

Metformin is generic and inexpensive. Most insurance plans cover it. A month's supply typically costs between $4 and $30 without insurance, depending on the dose and formulation.

Who should take it

Metformin is usually the first medication doctors prescribe for type 2 diabetes. Your doctor may recommend it if:

  • You have type 2 diabetes
  • You have prediabetes with risk factors
  • You are overweight

Important considerations

Metformin requires regular kidney function checks. It should not be used if your kidneys are severely impaired. Tell your doctor about all other medications you take.
