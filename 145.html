TRT Costs: The Real Numbers

Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) ranges from budget-friendly to expensive depending on your delivery method, dosage, and whether insurance picks up the tab.

Monthly Costs

Out-of-pocket TRT costs typically fall between $30 and $300 per month for the medication itself. Delivery method matters:

  • Injections: $50 to $200 monthly
  • Gels and creams: $100 to $400 monthly
  • Pellets: $200 to $400 per insertion (lasts 3-6 months)
  • Patches: $150 to $300 monthly

Injections generally cost less than topical options.

Annual Costs

Stack medication costs with clinic visits and lab work. Expect $1,500 to $4,000 yearly for TRT alone, before insurance:

  • Medication: $600 to $3,600
  • Initial consultations and bloodwork: $300 to $1,000
  • Ongoing monitoring appointments: $200 to $400
  • Lab tests (every 6-12 weeks): $200 to $600

What Insurance Covers

Most major insurers cover TRT when a doctor diagnoses clinically low testosterone (typically below 300 ng/dL). Coverage varies: some plans cover 70 to 90 percent of costs; others require prior authorization or limit which medications are covered.

Without Insurance

Telemedicine and fertility clinics often offer lower prices than traditional offices. Direct-pay programs range from $99 to $200 monthly for TRT plus basic monitoring.

The bottom line: Budget $50 to $300 monthly for medication plus $100 to $200 for appointments and labs if uninsured. Insurance usually brings your out-of-pocket costs under $50 monthly if your plan covers TRT.
