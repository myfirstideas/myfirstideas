Norelgestromin patch (Xulane)

Xulane is a contraceptive patch that delivers two hormones directly through your skin: norelgestromin (a progestin) and ethinyl estradiol (an estrogen). You apply one patch per week for three consecutive weeks, then skip a week before starting a new cycle.

How it works

The hormones prevent your ovaries from releasing an egg, which stops pregnancy from happening. The patch also thickens cervical mucus to add a second layer of protection.

Application and timing

Apply your patch to clean, dry skin on your abdomen, upper arm, or torso. Change it on the same day each week. If you forget to change your patch on schedule, effectiveness drops. During your patch-free week, you'll typically have a withdrawal bleed.

Effectiveness

When used perfectly, Xulane is over 99% effective at preventing pregnancy. Real-world effectiveness is around 91% because some users skip doses or apply patches incorrectly.

Side effects

Common side effects include breast tenderness, headache, nausea, and skin irritation at the patch site. Some people experience mood changes or decreased libido.

Who shouldn't use it

Xulane is not recommended for people over 40, smokers aged 35 and older, or those with a history of blood clots, heart problems, or migraine with aura.
