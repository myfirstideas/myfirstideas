What anastrozole is

Anastrozole is an aromatase inhibitor, a hormone therapy used to treat hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women. It works by blocking the enzyme aromatase, which converts other hormones into oestrogen in the body.

How it's used

Anastrozole is typically prescribed as adjuvant therapy (after surgery and chemotherapy) or as first-line treatment for advanced breast cancer. Standard dosing is 1 mg taken by mouth once daily.

Treatment duration varies: adjuvant therapy commonly runs 5 to 10 years, while metastatic disease treatment continues as long as the cancer responds.

Side effects and management

Common side effects include:

  • Joint and muscle pain (arthralgias and myalgias)
  • Hot flushes
  • Headache
  • Nausea
  • Fatigue
  • Vaginal dryness

Bone loss is a concern with long-term use. Your doctor may recommend bone density monitoring and preventive measures like calcium and vitamin D supplementation or bisphosphonates.

How anastrozole compares

Anastrozole belongs to the same class as letrozole and exemestane. Clinical trials show similar efficacy across these three aromatase inhibitors, so choice often depends on individual tolerance and side effect profiles.

Important considerations

Anastrozole is only effective in postmenopausal women. Premenopausal women typically receive tamoxifen or require ovarian suppression alongside an aromatase inhibitor.

Always discuss potential risks and benefits with your oncologist, particularly if you have a history of osteoporosis, cardiovascular disease, or thromboembolic events.
