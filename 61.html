Levonorgestrel is a synthetic progestin used in two ways: as emergency contraception (the morning-after pill) and as a regular birth control method.

Emergency Contraception

When taken as Plan B One-Step or generic equivalents, levonorgestrel works best within 72 hours of unprotected sex, though some effectiveness remains up to 120 hours. Timing matters: the sooner you take it, the better it works.

It's most effective for people under 165 pounds. Effectiveness decreases with body weight, and it doesn't work at all for people over 195 pounds; those individuals need a different emergency contraceptive.

Levonorgestrel is available over the counter without age restrictions or prescriptions.

Regular Birth Control

As an ongoing contraceptive, levonorgestrel appears in pills, patches and IUDs. When used consistently as directed, it prevents pregnancy in roughly 91 percent of typical users, though perfect use rates reach 99 percent.

Side Effects and Considerations

Common side effects include nausea, fatigue, headache and breast tenderness. Irregular bleeding patterns are typical, especially in the first few months. Most side effects settle within 2 to 3 cycles.

It doesn't protect against sexually transmitted infections.

Access

Emergency levonorgestrel is available at pharmacies, supermarkets and online retailers. Regular birth control formulations require a prescription in most cases.

If you're considering levonorgestrel or have questions about whether it's right for you, a telehealth provider can review your medical history and help you weigh your options.
