What propranolol does

Propranolol is a non-selective beta-blocker that reduces how hard your heart works and lowers blood pressure. It blocks the effects of adrenaline on your heart and blood vessels.

Common uses

Doctors prescribe propranolol for:

  • High blood pressure (hypertension)
  • Migraine prevention
  • Anxiety and performance anxiety
  • Heart palpitations
  • Tremors
  • Hyperthyroidism symptoms

How it works

Propranolol slows your heart rate and reduces the force of heart contractions. This lowers blood pressure and reduces oxygen demand on the heart. For migraines, it appears to prevent blood vessel changes that trigger attacks. For anxiety, it blocks the physical symptoms of adrenaline.

Dosing

Dosing varies by condition and individual tolerance. Immediate-release versions typically start at 40mg once or twice daily. Extended-release formulations allow once-daily dosing. Your doctor adjusts the dose based on your response and side effects.

Side effects

Common side effects include fatigue, dizziness, slow heart rate and cold hands or feet. Most are mild and improve with time. Some people experience vivid dreams or sexual dysfunction.

Important considerations

Do not stop propranolol suddenly. Abrupt withdrawal can cause rebound hypertension or heart problems. Tell your doctor about all other medications, especially other heart drugs. Propranolol may mask low blood sugar symptoms if you have diabetes.

Getting propranolol

Propranolol is available as a generic and is inexpensive. You need a prescription from a doctor or nurse practitioner. Telehealth providers can prescribe it for established conditions after evaluation.
