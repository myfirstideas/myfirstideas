Medicare Covers Telehealth, But Not Everything

Medicare pays for telehealth visits under specific conditions. The coverage isn't blanket, and the rules matter when you're deciding whether an online visit makes sense for your situation.

What Medicare Telehealth Services Include

Medicare Part B covers remote patient monitoring, virtual check-ins, and office visits delivered via telehealth. This includes mental health counseling, therapy, and psychiatric services. You can also get X-rays, lab work follow-ups and routine monitoring for chronic conditions online.

The service has to meet Medicare's definition: a real-time two-way audio and video interaction between you and a healthcare provider. Phone calls alone don't qualify. Text exchanges don't count either.

Your Costs Under Medicare

You pay the same copay or coinsurance for a telehealth visit as you would for the same visit in person. If your provider accepts Medicare assignment (most do), you pay 20 percent coinsurance after your deductible. The benefit rules are identical whether you're at home or in a clinic.

Eligibility Rules Still Apply

Being on Medicare doesn't mean every telehealth service is covered. Your provider has to be enrolled with Medicare. You need to have an established relationship with that provider or it has to be a first visit approved by Medicare rules. Some services still require an in-person evaluation first.

Check your coverage before booking. Coverage details vary by your specific Medicare plan, and Advantage plans can set their own telehealth rules within federal guidelines.
