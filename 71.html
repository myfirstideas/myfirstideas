Paxlovid Basics

Paxlovid is a combination antiviral medication containing nirmatrelvir and ritonavir. It's taken by mouth as tablets, typically over 5 days.

How It Works

Nirmatrelvir blocks a protein that the COVID virus needs to replicate. Ritonavir slows the breakdown of nirmatrelvir, keeping effective levels in your body longer.

Who Should Take It

Paxlovid is prescribed for adults with COVID who are at higher risk of severe illness. This includes people over 65, those with weakened immune systems, and people with chronic conditions like heart disease, diabetes, or lung disease.

Timeline

You must start Paxlovid within 5 days of COVID symptoms starting. The sooner you take it, the better it works.

Side Effects

Common side effects include altered taste, nausea, and diarrhoea. Most are mild and resolve after you finish the course.

Drug Interactions

Ritonavir affects how your body processes many other medications. Tell your doctor about everything you take, including supplements and over-the-counter drugs. Some combinations are unsafe.

Cost and Access

The US government has made Paxlovid available for free to uninsured and underinsured people. Check eligibility through your state health department or pharmacy.

Rebound COVID

A small number of people develop COVID symptoms again after completing Paxlovid. This is not treatment failure. Speak to your doctor if symptoms return.
