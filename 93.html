What is Tesamorelin?

Tesamorelin is a synthetic growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analog, a prescription injectable medication used specifically to treat HIV-related lipodystrophy: the abnormal fat distribution that occurs in some people with HIV.

It works by stimulating the body's natural growth hormone production, which helps reduce excess abdominal fat while preserving or increasing lean muscle mass.

Who Uses Tesamorelin

Tesamorelin is prescribed to people with HIV who have developed lipodystrophy, a condition where fat accumulates abnormally in the abdomen and other areas, or redistributes away from the face, arms and legs.

Not everyone with HIV develops this side effect, which was more common in earlier HIV treatment eras. Modern antiretroviral regimens cause it less frequently, though it still affects some patients.

How You Take It

Tesamorelin comes as an injection you give yourself once daily, typically in the abdomen. Your healthcare provider will show you proper injection technique during your first appointment.

Side Effects to Know About

Common side effects include:

  • Joint pain or swelling
  • Muscle pain
  • Carpal tunnel syndrome
  • Headache
  • Injection site reactions
  • Increased blood glucose levels

Some people experience water retention or swelling. Blood sugar monitoring is important, especially if you have diabetes or prediabetes.

Cost and Access

Tesamorelin is expensive, typically costing over $10,000 monthly without insurance. Coverage varies significantly by insurer, and some plans may require prior authorization or proof that lipodystrophy is substantially affecting your quality of life.

Patient assistance programs may be available through the manufacturer.

Questions to Ask Your Provider

  • Is tesamorelin appropriate given my current antiretroviral regimen?
  • What changes in body composition should I expect and over what timeframe?
  • How often will my blood glucose be monitored?
  • What happens if I stop taking it?
  • Are there patient assistance programs available if cost is a barrier?
