What is hCG?

hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) is a hormone your body produces during pregnancy. It's also used as a medication in two very different contexts: fertility treatment and weight loss.

The medication comes as an injection. Doctors prescribe it most commonly to treat infertility, especially in people with low testosterone or those undergoing assisted reproductive procedures. Its use for weight loss remains medically controversial.

How hCG Works

In fertility treatment, hCG mimics the natural hormone that triggers ovulation or stimulates sperm production. This is established medical practice.

For weight loss, supporters claim hCG suppresses appetite and preserves muscle during calorie restriction. Medical evidence for this mechanism is weak. The FDA has not approved hCG for weight loss, and major medical bodies do not recommend it for this purpose.

Cost and Access

hCG injections typically cost between 50 and 300 dollars per dose, depending on the pharmacy, dosage, and your insurance. Fertility clinics often include hCG as part of treatment packages.

You need a prescription. Telemedicine providers can prescribe hCG for fertility, though the injection itself must be administered at home or at a clinic. Some online weight loss programs offer hCG prescriptions, but this remains off-label and carries medical and legal questions.

Insurance Coverage

Most insurance plans cover hCG when prescribed for fertility problems. Coverage for weight loss is rare or nonexistent, since it's not an approved use.

Bottom Line

hCG is a proven hormone replacement for fertility issues. The evidence for weight loss is not solid, and regulatory bodies do not support that use. Talk to your doctor about whether hCG makes sense for your situation.
