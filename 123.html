5 Ways to Cut Prescription Costs Without Insurance

If you're paying out of pocket for medications, your pharmacy bill doesn't have to be astronomical. Here are five strategies that actually work.

1. Ask for the generic version

Generic drugs cost 80-85% less than brand-name alternatives and contain the same active ingredient. Your doctor can prescribe generics, or you can ask your pharmacist directly. Most common medications have a generic available.

2. Use free discount programs

GoodRx, SingleCare and RxSaver let you compare prices across pharmacies and apply digital coupons at checkout. No membership required. You typically save 20-70% on the listed price.

3. Check manufacturer coupons

Pharmaceutical companies offer patient assistance programs and coupons on their websites. These are free to access and can substantially reduce your cost per dose, especially for newer drugs.

4. Compare pharmacy prices

Prices vary wildly between pharmacies. A 30-day supply of one medication might cost $40 at one location and $120 at another. Price comparison tools show you the cheapest option nearby.

5. Visit a community health center

Federally qualified health centers (FQHCs) offer low-cost or sliding-scale medications. Call your local health department to find one in your area.

The math: combining a generic drug with a discount program often costs less than a brand-name drug with insurance coverage. Check prices before you pay full retail.
