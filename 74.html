What is Norethindrone?

Norethindrone is a synthetic progestin (a lab-made version of the hormone progesterone) used primarily in birth control pills and hormone replacement therapy. It works by preventing ovulation and thickening cervical mucus to reduce the chance of pregnancy.

Common Uses

Norethindrone appears in several medications:

  • Combination birth control pills: Often paired with ethinyl estradiol
  • Mini-pill (progestin-only pill): Used by people who cannot take estrogen
  • Hormone replacement therapy: For managing menopause symptoms
  • Endometriosis and abnormal bleeding: To reduce heavy periods

Dosage and How It Works

Dosage varies by formulation and purpose. For birth control, typical doses range from 0.35 mg to 1 mg daily. Your doctor will prescribe the strength that fits your health profile and contraceptive needs.

Norethindrone prevents pregnancy through multiple mechanisms: it stops the pituitary gland from releasing hormones that trigger ovulation, thickens cervical mucus to block sperm, and thins the uterine lining.

Side Effects

Common side effects include:

  • Nausea or breast tenderness (usually temporary)
  • Headaches
  • Spotting or breakthrough bleeding (especially with the mini-pill)
  • Mood changes
  • Weight changes

Severe side effects are rare but can include blood clots, stroke, or heart attack, particularly in people who smoke or have certain health conditions.

Effectiveness

When used perfectly, norethindrone-based pills are over 99% effective at preventing pregnancy. Real-world effectiveness is around 91% because missed pills reduce protection.

The progestin-only pill (mini-pill) is slightly less effective than combination pills if not taken at the exact same time daily.

Who Should Use It

Norethindrone suits people who need reliable contraception or hormone therapy. Those who cannot take estrogen often use the mini-pill form. However, it is not recommended for people with a history of blood clots, certain cancers, or uncontrolled high blood pressure.

Getting Norethindrone

Norethindrone requires a prescription. You can obtain it from a doctor's office visit or through telehealth appointments, where many providers can evaluate your eligibility and prescribe directly.
