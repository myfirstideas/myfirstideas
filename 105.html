Vardenafil is a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor, the same class of drug as sildenafil (Viagra) and tadalafil (Cialis). It treats erectile dysfunction by relaxing blood vessels in the penis and improving blood flow when sexual stimulation occurs.

How vardenafil works

Vardenafil blocks phosphodiesterase-5, an enzyme that restricts blood flow in the penis. By inhibiting this enzyme, the drug allows blood vessels to dilate and blood to flow more freely during sexual arousal. Without sexual stimulation, the drug does nothing.

Timing and onset

Vardenafil typically takes effect within 30 to 60 minutes, faster than sildenafil but comparable to tadalafil. Effects last 4 to 5 hours. Taking it on an empty stomach speeds absorption; fatty meals delay it.

Generic and brand versions

Vardenafil is available as a generic medication at lower cost. The brand-name version, Levitra, costs more but contains the identical active ingredient. Most insurance plans and telehealth providers cover the generic.

Dosing

Standard doses are 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg tablets. Most men start at 10 mg taken once daily as needed. Dose adjustments depend on effectiveness and side effects.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, flushing, indigestion, and nasal congestion. These are usually mild and temporary. Serious side effects are rare but include sudden vision loss and priapism (prolonged erection lasting more than 4 hours), which requires immediate medical attention.

Who should not take vardenafil

Vardenafil is unsafe for men taking nitrates (commonly prescribed for heart pain), men with uncontrolled high blood pressure, or those with a history of heart attack or stroke within 6 months. Always disclose your full medical history and current medications to your prescriber.
