How to get brand-name drugs for $0 through patient assistance programs

Patient assistance programs (PAPs) are manufacturer-run schemes that provide brand-name medications free or at reduced cost to people who qualify. Most major pharmaceutical companies offer them.

How patient assistance programs work

Drug manufacturers run these programs to help patients access their medications when cost is a barrier. You apply directly to the manufacturer or through a program like NeedyMeds or Patient Advocate Foundation. The company reviews your income, insurance status and medical need, then either approves you or doesn't.

If approved, you typically receive your medication directly from the manufacturer or a pharmacy they work with. There's usually no copay or a very small one.

Who qualifies

Eligibility varies by program and drug, but most check:

  • Household income (often below 200-400% of the federal poverty level)
  • Lack of insurance or underinsurance
  • U.S. citizenship or legal residency
  • Medical need for the specific drug

Having insurance doesn't automatically disqualify you if your copays are high.

How to apply

Find the manufacturer's PAP online, usually on their main website. You'll need proof of income (tax return, pay stub), proof of identity and a letter from your doctor confirming the prescription. Some programs let your doctor apply on your behalf.

Processing typically takes 1-4 weeks.

What to watch for

Programs can change eligibility requirements or close. Check directly with the manufacturer rather than relying on outdated third-party lists. Some programs require you to reapply annually or when your income changes.
