What Is GLP-1? The Hormone, the Drugs, and the Real Cost

GLP-1 receptor agonists have become the centre of conversations about diabetes treatment and weight management. But what is GLP-1, how do these drugs actually work, and what will they cost you?

The Hormone Behind the Drugs

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a hormone your gut naturally produces when you eat. It tells your pancreas to release insulin, slows your digestion, and signals your brain that you're full. People with type 2 diabetes don't produce enough of it, or their bodies don't respond to it properly.

GLP-1 receptor agonists are synthetic versions of this hormone. They mimic what your body should be doing on its own.

The Drugs

Common GLP-1 medications include:

  • Semaglutide (Ozempic for diabetes, Wegovy for weight loss)
  • Tirzepatide (Mounjaro for diabetes, Zepbound for weight loss)
  • Liraglutide (Victoza for diabetes, Saxenda for weight loss)
  • Dulaglutide (Trulicity)

You inject these weekly or daily, depending on the drug. They work for type 2 diabetes and weight loss, but they're not for type 1 diabetes and they carry real side effects: nausea, vomiting, pancreatitis risk, and a rare thyroid cancer signal.

The Real Cost

Without insurance, these drugs run 900 to 1,300 dollars per month. With insurance, your copay depends on your plan and your deductible. Some plans cover them for diabetes only, not weight loss. Some don't cover them at all.

Generic versions don't exist yet. Patient assistance programs exist, but eligibility is narrow. If you earn too much, you don't qualify.

The bottom line: GLP-1 drugs work, but access depends on your insurance, your diagnosis, and your income.
