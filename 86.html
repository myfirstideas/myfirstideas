What is sertraline?

Sertraline is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, or SSRI. It's one of the most commonly prescribed antidepressants in the US.

What sertraline treats

Doctors prescribe sertraline for:

  • Major depressive disorder
  • Generalized anxiety disorder
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)
  • Panic disorder
  • Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
  • Social anxiety disorder
  • Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD)

How it works

Sertraline increases serotonin in your brain by blocking its reabsorption. More serotonin in your system can ease symptoms of depression and anxiety.

What to expect

Sertraline typically takes 4 to 6 weeks to show full effect. Your doctor will usually start you on a lower dose and adjust it based on how you respond.

Common side effects include nausea, headache, insomnia, and sexual dysfunction. Most side effects improve or disappear within the first few weeks.

Getting sertraline via telehealth

Many online clinicians prescribe sertraline after an initial consultation. You'll answer questions about your medical history, current medications, and symptoms. Most telehealth visits take 30 to 60 minutes.

If your provider thinks sertraline is right for you, they'll send a prescription to your pharmacy. You can fill it at most major chains or through mail-order options.
