How Sumatriptan Works

Sumatriptan is a triptan, a class of medication designed to stop migraine pain rather than prevent it. It works by narrowing blood vessels around the brain and reducing substances in the body that can trigger headaches, nausea, sensitivity to light and sound, and other migraine symptoms.

Dosing and Delivery

Sumatriptan comes in several forms to match how you prefer to take it:

  • Oral tablets: 25mg, 50mg, or 100mg
  • Nasal spray: 11mg, 20mg, or 22mg
  • Subcutaneous injection: 6mg
  • Transdermal patch (iontophoretic): delivers medication through the skin

Most people take a single dose when a migraine starts. If your migraine returns or partially improves, you can take a second dose at least 2 hours after the first.

Effectiveness and Speed

Sumatriptan typically works within 30 minutes to 2 hours depending on the form you use. Injections act fastest. Patients report varying levels of relief, from complete pain cessation to partial improvement. Some find it works better for certain migraines than others.

Side Effects

Common side effects include tingling, flushing, dizziness, and chest tightness. Most are mild and short-lived. Serious side effects are rare but can include heart problems, so sumatriptan is not suitable for people with certain cardiovascular conditions.

Cost and Access

Sumatriptan is available as a generic medication, making it more affordable than brand-name alternatives. Most insurance plans cover it, though you may need a copay or to meet a deductible.

When to Use It

Sumatriptan works best when taken early in a migraine attack, ideally during the pain phase. It is not intended for daily use or migraine prevention. If you have frequent migraines, your doctor may suggest preventive medications instead.
