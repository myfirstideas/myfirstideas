Glutathione injections have become common in wellness clinics, but cost, safety and clinic quality vary wildly.

What You Pay

Glutathione injection costs typically range from $50 to $150 per session. Most people get them weekly or monthly, so annual spending can reach $2,400 to $7,200 depending on frequency and dosage. Some clinics bundle them with other IV therapies, which changes the price structure.

Side Effects to Know

Glutathione injections are generally well tolerated, but reported side effects include:

  • Rash or skin reactions at injection site
  • Nausea or mild stomach upset
  • Headache
  • Loose stools
  • Allergic reactions (rare but possible)

Most side effects are mild and temporary. Serious reactions are uncommon but can happen, especially in people with sulfa allergies or certain autoimmune conditions.

How to Vet a Clinic

Before booking, check these markers of clinic quality:

Licensing and credentials: The provider administering injections should be a licensed nurse, physician assistant or doctor. Verify their license with your state medical board.

Informed consent: Reputable clinics ask detailed health questions and have you sign consent forms that acknowledge limited clinical evidence for wellness uses.

Sourcing: Ask where the glutathione comes from. Compounded medications should come from a licensed pharmacy. Brand-name products should be clearly identified.

Pricing transparency: Red flags include vague pricing, pressure to buy packages upfront, or claims that injections cure disease.

Safety protocols: The clinic should use sterile technique, offer single-use vials, and not reuse needles or syringes.

If a clinic won't answer these questions directly, find another one.
