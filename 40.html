What is clomiphene?

Clomiphene is an oral medication used to treat infertility by stimulating ovulation. It works by blocking estrogen receptors in the brain, which triggers the pituitary gland to produce more follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH). These hormones prompt the ovaries to produce and release eggs.

How it's used

Clomiphene is taken by mouth, typically starting on day 3, 4, or 5 of your menstrual cycle. Standard dosing begins at 50 mg once daily for 5 days. If ovulation doesn't occur, your doctor may increase the dose in subsequent cycles, up to a maximum of 250 mg daily.

Timing matters: ovulation usually happens 5 to 10 days after your last dose.

Who it's for

Clomiphene is primarily prescribed to people assigned female at birth who have:

  • Irregular or absent ovulation
  • Unexplained infertility
  • Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
  • Hypothalamic amenorrhea

It's also sometimes used off-label in people assigned male at birth to address low testosterone and improve fertility.

Side effects

Common side effects include hot flashes, headaches, mood changes, bloating, and nausea. Most are mild and temporary. Rare but serious risks include ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) and ovarian cysts.

Cost and access

Clomiphene is affordable compared to other fertility treatments. Generic versions cost between 30 and 100 dollars per cycle without insurance. Many insurance plans cover it, particularly if infertility is documented. Some fertility clinics offer it as part of a larger treatment package.

Next steps

If you're considering clomiphene, discuss your medical history, current medications, and fertility goals with a doctor or fertility specialist. They'll monitor your response through blood tests and ultrasound.
