Peptides for Muscle Growth: How to Choose and What to Pay

Peptides marketed for muscle growth come with real differences in evidence, mechanism and cost. Here is what the numbers show.

What peptides actually do

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. Some bind to growth hormone releasing hormone (GHRH) receptors; others mimic insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). The effect on muscle depends on the peptide, the dose, the frequency and your training.

Not all peptides with muscle-building claims have the same research backing. Some show promise in animal or cell studies. Others have limited human data. Your choice should track the evidence, not the marketing.

Common peptides for muscle

Sermorelin: GHRH analog. Stimulates natural growth hormone release. More research on safety and tolerability in humans than most alternatives.

Ipamorelin: GHRH secretagogue. Selective for growth hormone. Animal studies suggest minimal cortisol elevation. Human efficacy data remain limited.

BPC-157: Body Protection Compound 157. Animal studies show tissue repair potential. Human clinical trials are sparse. Often sourced outside regulated channels.

TB-500: Thymosin beta-4 analog. Proposed for tissue healing and endurance. Animal evidence exists. Human safety and efficacy studies are not robust.

Pricing reality

Cost varies sharply by peptide, source and purity:

  • Sermorelin: $150 to $400 per month through legitimate telehealth providers offering peptide therapy
  • Ipamorelin: $200 to $500 per month
  • BPC-157: $50 to $200 per month through unregulated suppliers; cost is lower because regulation and verification are lower
  • TB-500: $100 to $300 per month through unregulated suppliers

Lower cost often signals lower oversight. Unregulated peptides carry risk of contamination, incorrect dosing and mislabeling.

How to choose

  1. Start with evidence: Sermorelin has the most human data. If you choose others, understand that you are relying on animal studies or anecdote.
  2. Verify source: Legitimate telehealth providers work with licensed pharmacies and provide lab testing results. Unregulated suppliers do not.
  3. Track cost against safety: Cheaper peptides from unknown sources may cost you later in side effects or ineffective treatment.
  4. Work with a provider: A clinician can assess your baseline hormone levels, monitor changes and adjust dose. Self-dosing carries higher risk of waste and harm.
  5. Expect modest results: Peptides are not steroids. Expect gradual gains in muscle mass and recovery if they work at all. Timelines are months, not weeks.

What you should ask a provider

  • Where does the peptide come from and what purity testing has it passed?
  • What is the expected timeline and how will you measure progress?
  • What are the real side effects at your proposed dose?
  • How often do you need to adjust dose and how will that be monitored?
  • What is the total cost including all consultations and lab work?

The bottom line

Peptides for muscle growth are not proven or approved by the FDA for this use. If you choose them, work with a telehealth provider offering legitimate peptide therapy, not with unregulated sellers. Expect to pay $150 to $500 per month. Expect results in months, not weeks. Ask questions until you understand what you are paying for and what the actual evidence says about it.
