How to Choose a GLP-1 Provider: Cost, Care and Red Flags

GLP-1 medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide work. The hard part is finding a provider who won't overcharge you or cut corners on safety.

What separates a good GLP-1 provider from a bad one

A responsible provider gives you upfront pricing, checks your medical history properly, and has a doctor actually involved in your care. Not all do.

Red flags to watch for

  • No clear pricing before you commit
  • Promises of rapid weight loss without medical assessment
  • Pressure to buy in bulk or commit to long treatment stretches
  • Minimal or no interaction with a real doctor
  • Unwillingness to discuss side effects or contraindications

What to ask about

  1. Medication cost: Get a number for the full month, including the provider fee. Compare it to your pharmacy price with insurance.
  2. Who prescribes: Is there a doctor involved, or just a nurse practitioner or physician assistant? Both can prescribe legally, but you want clear lines of medical responsibility.
  3. Ongoing monitoring: How often will you check in? Will they track your blood sugar, heart rate, or other relevant metrics?
  4. Refund or pause policy: What happens if you need to stop or if the medication doesn't work for you?

Cost reality

GLP-1s cost between 200 and 500 dollars per month through telehealth providers, depending on the medication and dose. Insurance coverage varies wildly. Your pharmacy price might beat a telehealth clinic's price, so check both.

The right fit

You want a provider who knows your medical history, answers questions directly, and prices honestly. That might be your primary care doctor, a telehealth clinic, or your local pharmacy. It won't be the one promising results without knowing anything about you.
