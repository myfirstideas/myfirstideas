What clindamycin does

Clindamycin is an antibiotic that stops bacterial growth by interfering with protein synthesis. It treats infections caused by susceptible bacteria, including some anaerobic organisms.

Common uses

Doctors prescribe clindamycin for:

  • Skin and soft tissue infections
  • Respiratory tract infections
  • Bone and joint infections
  • Certain parasitic infections
  • Acne (topical forms)
  • Bacterial vaginosis
  • Toxoplasmosis (in combination therapy)

How to take it

Clindamycin comes as capsules, liquid, topical lotions, foams, and gels. Take oral forms with a full glass of water. Dosage depends on the type and severity of infection: typically 150 to 450 mg every 6 to 8 hours for adults. Follow your prescription exactly.

Side effects to know about

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and skin irritation (topical use). A serious but rare side effect is Clostridioides difficile infection, which can cause severe diarrhea and colitis. Report persistent diarrhea to your doctor immediately.

Drug interactions

Clindamycin interacts with:

  • Neuromuscular blocking agents (increased paralysis risk)
  • Erythromycin (reduced clindamycin effectiveness)
  • Certain oral anticoagulants (altered blood thinner effects)

Tell your doctor about all medications you take before starting clindamycin.

Important notes

Tell your doctor if you have a history of colitis or Clostridioides difficile infection. Clindamycin can reduce the effectiveness of certain birth control pills. Use backup contraception if needed. Finish the full course even if you feel better.
