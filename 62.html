What is liraglutide?

Liraglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist, a class of medication that works by mimicking a natural hormone your body produces. It slows digestion, reduces hunger signals, and helps control blood sugar levels.

You inject liraglutide once a week under the skin. Two brand names deliver it: Saxenda for weight management and Victoza for type 2 diabetes.

How it works

Liraglutide attaches to GLP-1 receptors in your brain and gut. This triggers satiety, meaning you feel full longer and eat less. It also slows how fast your stomach empties, keeping you satisfied between meals.

For diabetes, it reduces glucose production and helps your pancreas release insulin when blood sugar is high.

Common side effects

Nausea is most frequent, especially when starting. Other patients report vomiting, constipation, or diarrhoea. Side effects often decrease after the first few weeks as your body adjusts.

Rarely, liraglutide can cause pancreatitis or gallbladder problems. Tell your doctor immediately if you experience severe abdominal pain.

Cost and coverage

Liraglutide is expensive without insurance, typically ranging from 900 to 1,500 dollars per month depending on dosage. Most insurance plans cover it for type 2 diabetes. Coverage for weight loss varies; some plans require prior authorisation or proof that you have tried other treatments first.

GoodRx and similar discount programs can reduce costs significantly if you pay out of pocket.

Getting started

Your doctor will prescribe a starting dose and show you how to inject. You begin low and increase gradually over weeks to reach your target dose. This ramp-up reduces nausea.

You'll need follow-up appointments to check how well it is working and monitor for side effects.
