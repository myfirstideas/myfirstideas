Buying Prescriptions From a Canadian Pharmacy: What You Need to Know

Canadian pharmacies often advertise significantly lower prices than US pharmacies. For people paying out of pocket, the savings can be real. But buying prescriptions across the border involves legal restrictions, insurance complications and safety questions worth understanding.

Price differences are substantial

Canadian pharmacies typically charge 40 to 70 percent less for the same medications sold in the United States. The gap exists because Canada regulates drug prices; the US does not. A month's supply of a common statin might cost $150 in the US and $40 in Canada.

Legal status is complicated

Federal law allows US citizens to import a personal 90-day supply of most medications for personal use, provided they have a valid prescription. You cannot buy controlled substances (Schedule II through V) from Canadian pharmacies and import them legally. State laws vary on what's permitted, and some states restrict imports more strictly than federal law does.

Insurance won't cover it

If you buy from a Canadian pharmacy, your US insurance will not pay for those medications. You'll pay the full price yourself, even if you have excellent coverage. This matters if you're comparing the Canadian price against your copay, not against the full US retail price.

Verify the pharmacy is legitimate

Not all online pharmacies claiming to be Canadian are actually licensed in Canada. The National Association of Boards of Pharmacy (NABP) maintains a list of verified Canadian pharmacies through its VIPPS program. Buying from an unlicensed pharmacy carries serious risks: counterfeit medications, contaminated products or no product at all.

Safety and supply chain questions remain

Canadian pharmacies are regulated by provincial bodies, and standards are generally high. But medications purchased online bypass some of the verification steps that in-person pharmacies provide. You won't have a pharmacist who knows your full medication history or can flag dangerous interactions.

Consider alternatives first

Before ordering from Canada, ask your doctor about generic versions, patient assistance programs, or switching to a medication your insurance covers at a lower copay. GoodRx and similar discount programs sometimes match or beat Canadian prices for uninsured customers. Some employers and nonprofits negotiate bulk discounts that may be available to you.
